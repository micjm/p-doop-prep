## 1. Toby Ord, *The Precipice* (2020): the risk table, and whether Ord updated it ("The Precipice Revisited", 2024)

### Takeaway
Ord puts total existential risk over the next century (2021–2120) at about 1 in 6. Unaligned AI is 1 in 10, engineered pandemics 1 in 30, nuclear war and climate change 1 in 1,000 each, natural pandemics 1 in 10,000, and all natural risks combined about 1 in 10,000. In his "Precipice Revisited" talk (EAG Bay Area, 4 Feb 2024; posted 12 Jul 2024) he gave **no new numbers**. Qualitatively, climate risk is down, nuclear risk is up, and pandemics and AI are "mixed" with no clear overall direction.

### Cited Findings
- Ord estimates "a 1 in 6 total risk of existential catastrophe occurring in the next century", mostly anthropogenic, with natural risks such as asteroids "relatively negligible". He puts unaligned AGI at 1 in 10, "higher than all other sources of existential risk combined". — [Wikipedia: The Precipice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precipice:_Existential_Risk_and_the_Future_of_Humanity)
- Ord's own 2024 restatement: "In *The Precipice*, I gave my best guess probabilities for the existential risk over the next 100 years… These were 1 in 1,000 for Climate and for Nuclear, 1 in 30 for Pandemics and 1 in 10 for Unaligned AI." — [Toby Ord, "The Precipice Revisited" (talk 4 Feb 2024, posted 12 Jul 2024)](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited)
- The update in Ord's words: "these are all rounded off (usually to the nearest factor of 10) and none of them have moved *that* far… in my view, Climate risk is down, Nuclear is up, and the story on Pandemics and AI is mixed — lots of changes, but no clear direction for the overall risk." — [Ord 2024](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited)
- Reasons for the changes, per Ord 2024:
  - **Climate down:** emissions are tracking between the lower RCPs, not RCP 8.5, and IPCC AR6 narrowed the likely climate sensitivity from 1.5–4.5°C to 2.5–4°C.
  - **Nuclear up:** the Ukraine war, New START expiring with a likely new arms race, and the MacArthur Foundation (about 45% of field funding) withdrawing.
  - **Pandemics mixed:** COVID exposed institutional weakness, but mRNA vaccines arrived in about 1 year, metagenomic sequencing and far-UVC are promising, and AI is lowering the barrier to bioweapons.
  - **AI mixed:** the shift from RL agents to LLMs, racing between big tech firms (bad), and governance momentum such as the Bletchley Declaration and AI Safety Institutes (good).
  
  — [Ord 2024](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited)
- The FRI/XPT comparison table lists these Ord figures for existential catastrophe by 2120: engineered pathogens 3.3%, natural pathogens 0.01%, AI 10%, nuclear 0.1%, non-anthropogenic 0.01%, total 16.67%. — [FRI, "What do XPT forecasts tell us about AI risk?" (EA Forum, 19 Jul 2023)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Ord rates the Cuban Missile Crisis, when leaders put nuclear war at 10–50%, as the closest humanity has come to self-destruction. — [Wikipedia: The Precipice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precipice:_Existential_Risk_and_the_Future_of_Humanity)
- Ord says humanity spends under 0.001% of gross world product on targeted existential-risk reduction. — [Wikipedia: The Precipice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precipice:_Existential_Risk_and_the_Future_of_Humanity)

### Inferences
- Annualised (computed): 1/6 per century ≈ 0.18% per year; 1/10 per century ≈ 0.105% per year; 1/1,000 per century ≈ 0.001% per year.
- For the website, Ord's 2020 numbers are still the author-endorsed current values as of his last public statement (2024). Climate could reasonably be shown slightly below 1/1,000 and nuclear slightly above, but Ord declined to give replacement figures. Any new numbers the site shows should be labelled as the site's own interpretation.
- Ord's figures are for *existential* catastrophe, which includes unrecoverable collapse and permanent dystopia. XPT figures (section 3) are for *extinction* (<5,000 humans) or *catastrophe* (>10% die within 5 years). The two should not be compared one-to-one.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full *Precipice* Table 6.1 from a primary or reliable secondary source in this session. These values are widely reproduced but **unverified here**:
  - asteroid/comet ≈1 in 1,000,000
  - supervolcano ≈1 in 10,000
  - stellar explosion ≈1 in 1,000,000,000
  - other environmental damage ≈1 in 1,000
  - unforeseen anthropogenic ≈1 in 30
  - other anthropogenic ≈1 in 50
  - total anthropogenic ≈1 in 6
  
  Verify against the book (p. 167, Table 6.1) before publishing.
- Ord has published no numeric update since February 2024 that I could find. His Jan 2025 essay "The Scaling Paradox" is on AI scaling, not risk tables.

## 2. 2008 FHI Global Catastrophic Risks Conference informal survey (Sandberg & Bostrom)

### Takeaway
The 2008 FHI informal survey (FHI Technical Report 2008-1) is confirmed to exist and is cited by FRI and Wikipedia. Its AI figure, a 5% chance of extinction from superintelligence by 2100, is confirmed by two secondary sources. I could not retrieve the PDF to confirm the 19% overall-extinction headline or the rest of the table.

### Cited Findings
- "A 2008 survey by the Future of Humanity Institute estimated a 5% probability of extinction by superintelligence by 2100." Cited to Bostrom & Sandberg (2008), "Global Catastrophic Risks Survey", FHI Technical Report #2008-1. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- FRI lists "Sandberg and Bostrom, 2100, 5%" for AI extinction, and the same 5% for AI catastrophe, in its comparison table. — [FRI on EA Forum, 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)

A Wayback capture exists (288 captures, 31 Oct 2008 – 17 Aug 2025) but would not render as text. — [Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20210111211647/https://www.fhi.ox.ac.uk/reports/2008-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The survey was an informal poll of conference attendees, a self-selected group of risk researchers. It is better treated as a historical marker than as calibrated evidence.

### Gaps

  - superintelligent AI 5%

  - nuclear terrorism 0.03%
  
  Commonly reproduced columns also include "≥1 million dead" and "≥1 billion dead". Check the archived PDF before using any of these except AI 5%.

## 3. XPT: Forecasting Research Institute (2023), "Forecasting Existential Risk: Evidence from a Long-Run Forecasting Tournament"

### Takeaway
The XPT ran June–October 2022, with forecasts frozen on 31 Oct 2022. It had 169 participants: 80 superforecasters and 89 experts. (The FRI landing page says 80 experts and 89 superforecasters, so the two FRI pages swap the group sizes.) For 2100, median superforecasters put **total catastrophe at 9.05% and extinction at 1%**. Median domain experts put **catastrophe at 20% and extinction at 6%**. AI showed the largest disagreement: extinction 0.38% (superforecasters) vs 3% (experts).

### Cited Findings
- Design: XPT ran June–October 2022 and forecasts stopped updating on 31 Oct 2022. Definitions: catastrophe = ">10% of humans die within 5 years"; extinction = "human population <5,000". — [FRI, "What do XPT forecasts tell us about AI risk?" (19 Jul 2023)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Headline: "The median domain expert predicted a 20% chance of catastrophe and a 6% chance of human extinction by 2100. The median superforecaster predicted a 9% chance of catastrophe and a 1% chance of extinction." — [FRI announcement, EA Forum (10 Jul 2023)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Superforecaster medians (FRI table; Ord's 2120 *existential* figures in the last column for comparison) — [FRI 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT):

| Risk (superforecaster median) | Catastrophe 2030 | Catastrophe 2050 | Catastrophe 2100 | Extinction 2030 | Extinction 2050 | Extinction 2100 | Ord, existential by 2120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biological (total) | – | – | 1.8% | – | – | 0.012% | – |
| Engineered pathogens | – | – | 0.8% | – | – | 0.01% | 3.3% |
| Natural pathogens | – | – | 1% | – | – | 0.0018% | 0.01% |
| AI | 0.01% | 0.73% | 2.13% | 0.0001% | 0.03% | 0.38% | 10% |
| Nuclear | 0.50% | 1.83% | 4% | 0.001% | 0.01% | 0.074% | 0.1% |
| Non-anthropogenic | 0.0026% | 0.015% | 0.05% | 0.0004% | 0.0014% | 0.0043% | 0.01% |
| **Total** | 0.85% | 3.85% | **9.05%** | 0.01% | 0.3% | **1%** | 16.67% |

- AI domain experts (medians; n=30 for catastrophe, n=29 for extinction):
  - Catastrophe: 0.35% (2030), 5% (2050), 12% (2100).
  - Extinction: 0.02% (2030), 1.1% (2050), 3% (2100).
  
  — [FRI 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Forecasters in the top reciprocal-scoring quintile were lower still:
  - Superforecasters (n=16): AI catastrophe 1.82% and AI extinction 0.088% by 2100.
  - Domain experts (n=5): AI catastrophe 3% and AI extinction 0.24% by 2100.
  
  "The better forecasters were at discerning what other people would predict, the less concerned they were about extinction." — [FRI 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Superforecasters put *catastrophic* risk from nuclear by 2100 at almost twice that from AI (4% vs 2.13%). For *extinction*, AI was about 10× nuclear, and nuclear about 10× non-anthropogenic. — [FRI 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Nuclear extinction by 2100: domain experts 0.55% vs superforecasters 0.074%. This comes from a commenter (Vasco Grilo) citing the XPT report, so it is secondary. — [EA Forum comment on FRI announcement](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Expert sample: 32 AI, 15 general x-risk, 12 bio, 12 nuclear and 9 climate experts. About 42% of experts and 9% of superforecasters had attended an EA meetup. Dropout was 19% for superforecasters and 51% for experts (as reported by a commenter). — [EA Forum comments quoting XPT report](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Framing effects in the public survey (912 college graduates), quoting the report:
  - The median for extinction by 2100 was 5% when asked for a percentage, but **1 in 15 million** when asked for "1-in-X" odds with low-probability reference examples.
  - AI extinction was 2% under the percentage framing vs 1 in 30 million under the odds framing.
  
  — [EA Forum comment quoting XPT p. 9](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Published version: Karger et al., "Subjective-probability forecasts of existential risk: Initial results from a hybrid persuasion-forecasting tournament", *International Journal of Forecasting* (FRI page dated 17 Jan 2025). FRI also released "Assessing Near-Term Accuracy in the XPT" (2 Sep 2025) and runs the ongoing LEAP expert AI panel. — [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/subjective-probability-forecasts-of-existential-risk-initial-results-from-a-hybrid-persuasion-forecasting-tournament); [FRI XPT page](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt)

### Inferences
- Superforecasters' natural-pathogen catastrophe estimate (1% by 2100) is larger than their engineered-pathogen estimate (0.8%). For extinction it reverses: engineered 0.01% vs natural 0.0018%. They judge natural pandemics plausible as mass-death events but much less likely to be extinction-level.
- Non-anthropogenic extinction (0.0043% by 2100, about 78 years from 2022) works out to about 0.0055% per century (computed). That is well inside the fossil-record upper bound (section 10) and close to Ord's 0.01%.

### Gaps
- I could not get domain-expert medians for engineered pathogens, natural pathogens or non-anthropogenic risk, or the full-sample expert totals by cause, because the 754-page PDF exceeded the fetch limit. The source tables are in the XPT PDF (pp. 133–143 per FRI's links).
- FRI has run no repeat of the full XPT since the October 2022 freeze that I could find.

## 4. Other aggregate sources: Stern, national risk registers, GCF, Metaculus, Doomsday Clock, Rees, others

### Takeaway
- Doomsday Clock: 85 seconds to midnight (27 Jan 2026), the closest ever; it was 89 seconds in 2025.
- Metaculus: the community put human extinction by 2100 at about 2% (as of 29 Aug 2026, via Wikipedia).
- Global Challenges Foundation (2016): at least 0.05% per year.
- Several other headline figures (Stern 0.1%/yr, Rees 50%, UK NRR likelihoods, Riley 12%) could not be verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Doomsday Clock:** "On January 27, 2026, the Doomsday Clock was set at 85 seconds to midnight, the closest the Clock has ever been to midnight in its history." It moved from 89 seconds (2025); earlier settings were 90 s (2023), 100 s (2020), 2 min (2018) and 2.5 min (2017). The Bulletin's 2026 calls cover nuclear arsenals, AI guidelines and biological threats. — [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists](https://thebulletin.org/doomsday-clock/)
- **Metaculus:** "As of August 29, 2026, Metaculus users estimate a 2% probability of human extinction by 2100." — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction), citing [Metaculus "Will humans become extinct by 2100?"](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/578/human-extinction-by-2100/)
- **Metaculus Ragnarök series (as of mid-2023):** AI catastrophe by 2100 3.99%; AI extinction by 2100 1.9%. — [FRI table citing Metaculus Ragnarök results](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT); [Metaculus Ragnarök "results so far" page](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/2568/ragnar%C3%B6k-seriesresults-so-far/) (page rendered without content)
- **Global Challenges Foundation 2016 annual report:** "an annual probability of human extinction of at least 0.05% per year (equivalent to 5% per century, on average)". — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction), citing [The Atlantic, 29 Apr 2016](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2016/04/a-human-extinction-isnt-that-unlikely/480444/)
- **UNDRR (secondary):** "As the United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction estimated in 2023, there is a 2 to 14% chance of an extinction-level event by 2100." Wikipedia cites this via The Atlantic (Brian Klaas, 12 Mar 2025); I have not checked it against a UNDRR primary document. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **Bostrom (2002):** setting the probability of existential catastrophe below 25% "would be misguided, and the best estimate may be considerably higher". — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **John Leslie (1996):** 70% chance humanity survives the next five centuries, partly based on the doomsday argument. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **J. Richard Gott (1993):** 95% probability of extinction within about 8 million years, from the doomsday argument. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **AEI / Roger Pielke Jr. (13 Nov 2024):** probability of nuclear war in the 21st century "between 0% and 80%"; supervolcanic eruption about 0.0067% per year (0.67% per century). — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **The Economist (10 Jul 2023), reporting XPT-type figures:** 12% AI catastrophe and 3% AI extinction by 2100, with nuclear 8% catastrophe and 0.5625% extinction. These match XPT domain-expert medians. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **Martin Rees, *Our Final Hour* (2003):** argues the 21st century may be when humanity's fate is decided. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **UK National Risk Register 2025:** published by the Cabinet Office on 16 Jan 2025 (187-page PDF). It is the external version of the National Security Risk Assessment and is the first NRR aligned with the NSRA's new "dynamic" reassessment process. — [GOV.UK: National Risk Register 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-risk-register-2025)

### Inferences
- Implied total-risk figures per century, lowest to highest:
  - XPT superforecasters: about 1% extinction.
  - Metaculus: about 2% extinction.
  - GCF: about 5% (0.05%/yr).
  - XPT experts: 6% extinction.
  - Stern's working assumption, if verified: about 9.5% (0.1%/yr; computed).
  - Ord: 16.7% existential.
  - Rees: 50% (unverified).
  
  That is a factor of about 50 between low and high, and definitions differ: extinction vs existential vs "civilisation ends".
- The Doomsday Clock is a symbolic indicator, not a probability, and should not be turned into a slider value.

## 5. Asteroid/comet impact: NASA planetary defense, DART, survey completeness

### Takeaway
Impacts large enough to be globally catastrophic (≥1 km) happen about once per 440,000–500,000 years. That is about 0.02% per century, and over 90% of such NEAs have been found, none on a collision course. Regional-scale impacts (≥140 m) happen about once per 30,000 years (about 0.33% per century), but only about 40–45% of such objects have been found. DART (2022) showed that kinetic deflection works.

### Cited Findings
- Impact recurrence (one widely used model):
  - ≥4 m: about once a year.
  - 7 m (Hiroshima-scale): every 5 years.
  - 60 m (Tunguska-scale, 10 Mt): every 1,300 years. Other estimates range from one every 300 to every 2,000–3,000 years.
  - 1 km: every 440,000 years.
  - 5 km: every 18 million years.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- Snyder-Beattie et al. Table 3: asteroid ≥1 km about once per 500 kyr (Napier 2008); ≥5 km once per 6 Myr (Chapman & Morrison 1994). Impacts dispersing enough dust to significantly disrupt photosynthesis occur about every 15 Myr (Toon et al. 1997). — [Snyder-Beattie, Ord & Bonsall 2019, Table 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7/tables/3); [main text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- Some researchers argue that about 1 km impacts "would fall below the threshold for a global catastrophe" (Toon et al. 1997). — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- **≥1 km survey:** Congress's 1998 mandate was to find 90% of NEAs ≥1 km by 2008. Discovery reached about 20% (1998), 65% (2004), 80% (2006) and 93% (2011), so the goal was met three years late. Estimated population: 981±19 (2021 study). As of Dec 2024, 867 NEAs ≥1 km were catalogued. — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- **≥140 m survey:**
  - The 2005 George E. Brown Act set a goal of 90% by 2020; it was missed.
  - Completeness was "about half" (Sept 2020 estimate), then 38% (Dec 2023 estimate). A 2023 study put it at 44% of an estimated ~35,000 by end-2022, using H=23 as the size proxy.
  - The Vera C. Rubin Observatory, surveying since 2025, is expected to raise this to ≥60%. NEO Surveyor (launch 2027) is expected to reach 76% during its 5-year mission.
  - Objects this size hit Earth about once per 30,000 years.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- **Catalogue (CNEOS, 30 Dec 2024):** 37,378 NEOs (123 comets); 2,465 potentially hazardous asteroids; 11,167 known NEAs >140 m. — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- **DART:** on 26 Sep 2022, DART hit Dimorphos (moon of Didymos). It shortened Dimorphos's orbital period by 33 minutes, with momentum transfer 3.6× the spacecraft's own momentum (mostly from ejecta). ESA's Hera (launched Oct 2024) is due to arrive at Didymos in December 2026. China plans a deflection test on 2015 XF261 in 2027. — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object) (citing NASA press releases of 27 Sep and 15 Dec 2022)
- **Recent scares:**
  - 2024 YR4 (about 55 m) reached Torino 3. Its 22 Dec 2032 impact probability peaked at 1 in 32 (Feb 2025), then fell to 1 in 120,000 (Mar 2025). It had about a 4% chance of hitting the Moon.
  - Apophis was removed from the Sentry risk list in Feb 2021.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- **Long-period comets:** threat estimated at most 1% of the NEA threat, but with only a few years' warning. Salotti (2022) puts a giant comet impact in the next 100 years at 2.2×10⁻¹². — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object); [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **Extinction-level impact winter:** an impact large enough to cause human extinction before 2100 has been estimated at one in a million (Matheny 2007). — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- **Impacts on ordinary people:**
  - Chelyabinsk (15 Feb 2013): a 20 m object exploded with 400–500 kt yield over a city.
  - 140 m+ impacts cause "large-scale, though not global" damage.
  - ≥1 km impacts are "sufficient to cause a global catastrophe".
  
  — [Wikipedia: Near-Earth object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_object)
- A minor impact could be mistaken for a nuclear attack and trigger retaliation (Baum 2018). — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

### Inferences
- Per-century equivalents (computed):

| Impactor | Recurrence | Chance per century |
|---|---|---|
| ≥1 km | 440–500 kyr | ≈0.020–0.023% |
| ≥5 km | 6–18 Myr | ≈0.0006–0.0017% |
| ≥140 m | 30 kyr | ≈0.33% |
| Tunguska-class | 1,300 yr | ≈7.4% |

- Most ≥1 km objects are already catalogued and none threaten Earth in the next century, so the *residual* risk is lower than the raw base rate. The remainder is mainly undiscovered objects and long-period comets.
- Suggested slider default: asteroid existential risk about 1 in 1,000,000 per century, matching Matheny and (unverified) Ord. Global-catastrophe-level impacts (≥1 km) at about 1 in 5,000 per century as the base rate, lower after survey discounting.

### Gaps
- I could not reach NASA PDCO/CNEOS pages directly (404s). Survey completeness figures come from Wikipedia's summary of NASA/CNEOS and the 2021/2023 studies.
- No NAS or Lloyd's damage estimate for asteroid impacts was retrieved.

## 6. Supervolcanic eruptions (VEI-8)

### Takeaway
Recurrence estimates for super-eruptions conflict by about two orders of magnitude. Rougier et al. (2018) give about 17,000 years for eruptions ≥10³ km³, or about 0.6% per century. Older estimates give about 50,000 years, or 1.1 Myr in Mason et al. (2004). Most such eruptions would probably not threaten extinction. Toba (about 74,000 years ago) shows no clear sign of a human bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- Rougier, Sparks, Cashman & Brown (2018, *EPSL* 482:621–629) estimate a recurrence time of **17 kyr** for eruptions ejecting >10³ km³. That is higher than the paper's own conservative extinction bound of 6.9×10⁻⁵/yr. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- The same table also lists "Supervolcano 10³ km³: 1.1 Myr" (Mason, Pyle & Oppenheimer 2004) next to the 17 kyr Rougier figure, which shows how much estimates vary. Flood basalts occur about every 32 Myr (Rampino & Stothers 1988). — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019, Table 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7/tables/3)
- "The geological record suggests that supervolcanic eruptions are estimated to occur on average about once every 50,000 years, though most such eruptions would not reach the scale required to cause human extinction" (Rampino & Ambrose 2002). — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- AEI (Pielke, 13 Nov 2024): annual supervolcanic eruption probability about 0.0067% (0.67% per century). — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- Toba (about 74 kya) was "the most severe eruption of the past 2 million years", but evidence for a human bottleneck is contested:
  - Lake Malawi records show no volcanic winter in East Africa.
  - South African communities "thrived" through it.
  - Most hominids show no bottleneck, with possible exceptions in eastern chimpanzees and Sumatran orangutans.
  
  Flood-basalt events (about every 20–30 Myr) are the volcanic events linked to mass extinctions. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

### Inferences
- Per-century equivalents (computed): about 0.59% for a 17 kyr recurrence; about 0.2% for 50 kyr; about 0.009% for 1.1 Myr.
- For ordinary people, a VEI-8 eruption is mainly a **global food-supply shock** (volcanic winter, years of cooling and crop failure) rather than an extinction threat. Suggested website framing: "catastrophe (mass famine) ~0.2–0.6%/century; extinction far lower".
- If Ord's (unverified) 1 in 10,000 existential figure is right, he judges that only about 1–5% of super-eruptions would become existential.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the Rougier et al. 2018 abstract (wrong article ID at ScienceDirect), so I could not check its central estimate or credible interval. Values commonly quoted from it, unverified here: about 17 kyr for M8, with a 95% interval of roughly 5.2–48 kyr.
- No quantitative estimate of super-eruption *fatalities* or food-system impacts (e.g., ALLFED modelling) was retrieved.

## 7. Extreme space weather: Carrington-level storms and grid collapse

### Takeaway
A Carrington-class storm is a high-probability, civilisation-disrupting but non-existential risk. Ice cores suggest events of similar intensity recur about once per 500 years. A July 2012 Carrington-class CME missed Earth by about nine days. Lloyd's (2013) estimated US damages of $0.6–2.6 trillion. Whether transformers would be destroyed is disputed (Metatech vs NERC).

### Cited Findings
- **The 1859 Carrington Event:** the most intense geomagnetic storm on record, with Dst estimated at −0.80 to −1.75 μT (about −1,760 nT). Telegraphs failed across Europe and North America, operators were shocked and some stations caught fire. A 2024 digitisation of Kew/Greenwich magnetograms found rates of change >700 nT/min, about twice the modern 1-in-100-year extreme of 350–400 nT/min at that latitude. — [Wikipedia: Carrington Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event); [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm)
- **Recurrence:** "Ice cores show evidence that events of similar intensity recur at an average rate of approximately once per 500 years." This is uncited in Wikipedia, and later ice-core work casts doubt on nitrate-based solar proxies. — [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm); contradicted on method by [Wikipedia: Carrington Event, "Other evidence"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event)
- **Near miss:** "On 23 July 2012, a 'Carrington-class' solar superstorm… was observed, but its trajectory narrowly missed Earth by a margin of roughly nine days." — [Wikipedia: Carrington Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event), citing [NASA (23 Jul 2014)](https://science.nasa.gov/science-news/science-at-nasa/2014/23jul_superstorm/)
- **Larger historical events:** the 774–775 CE (¹⁴C) event was "10 or more times the size of the Carrington Event". Another occurred in 993–994. A 7176 BCE event may have exceeded 774–775. — [Wikipedia: Carrington Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event)
- **Lloyd's of London and AER (June 2013):** a Carrington-level event today would cost the US $600 billion to $2.6 trillion (about $0.79–3.44 trillion in 2024 dollars), or about 3.6–15.5% of annual US GDP at the time. — [Wikipedia: Carrington Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event), citing [Lloyd's/AER, "Solar Storm Risk to the North American Electric Grid" (2013)](https://www.lloyds.com/news-and-insights/risk-reports/library/natural-environment/solar-storm)
- **National Academies (2008), *Severe Space Weather Events*:** a Carrington-scale storm could cause "billions or even trillions of dollars of damage" and "electrical blackouts on a massive scale that might not be repaired for weeks, months, or even years". — [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm)
- **Metatech (2008):** a 1921-strength storm would destroy more than 300 transformers and leave over 130 million Americans without power, costing several trillion dollars. **Contradicted by NERC (2012),** which found "temporary grid instability but no widespread destruction of high-voltage transformers". NERC notes the 1989 Québec collapse was caused by relay tripping, not transformer burnout. — [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm)
- **March 1989 storm:** Hydro-Québec collapsed in seconds and 6 million people lost power for 9 hours. In 2016 FERC adopted geomagnetic-disturbance reliability standards, with upgrades required within four years. — [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm)
- **Food impacts (Moersdorf et al. 2024, *Global Challenges*):** in a global catastrophic infrastructure-loss scenario, losing industrial inputs could cut crop yields 38–48% globally, and up to 75% in places like Central Europe. — [Wikipedia: Carrington Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_Event)
- **Grid geography:** long high-voltage lines (North America, China, Australia) are most vulnerable; Europe's shorter circuits are less so. A superstorm could also cause months-long global Internet outages (Jyothi 2021). — [Wikipedia: Geomagnetic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetic_storm)

### Inferences
- If Carrington-class storms recur about once per 500 years: about 2% per decade and about 18% per century (computed). The commonly cited Riley (2012) figure of about 12% per decade (unverified, see Gaps) implies about 72% per century. That gap is why later revisions matter.
- For ordinary people the main impacts are multi-day to multi-month regional blackouts, GPS and communications loss, and knock-on effects on water, fuel, payments and food supply chains. It is a GCR-adjacent (economic/civilisational) risk with essentially zero direct extinction probability, so it suits a "catastrophe" slider better than an "extinction" slider.

### Gaps
- **Riley (2012), *Space Weather* 10, S02012, "On the probability of occurrence of extreme space weather events":** the abstract was blocked (Wiley 403). The commonly quoted ~12% chance of a Carrington-level storm per decade is unverified here.
- Later revisions (e.g., Riley & Love 2017, Love 2012/2015, Chapman et al. 2020, Moriña et al. 2019) commonly give lower per-decade probabilities, roughly 0.5%–4%; none were retrieved in this session.
- UK NRR 2025 likelihood for "severe space weather" could not be extracted (PDF binary).

## 8. Gamma-ray bursts, supernovae and other cosmic risks

### Takeaway
Stellar explosions dangerous enough to cause mass extinction are estimated at once per 100 Myr (supernovae) and once per 170 Myr (GRBs). That is about 10⁻⁶ per century or less, effectively negligible on human timescales. Vacuum collapse is bounded at more than 1 Gyr.

### Cited Findings
- Snyder-Beattie et al. (2019) Table 3 frequencies — [Nature Sci. Rep. Table 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7/tables/3):
  - gamma-ray burst: one per 170 Myr (Melott et al. 2004, linked to the late Ordovician extinction hypothesis)
  - supernovae: one per 100 Myr (Beech 2011)
  - vacuum collapse: more than 1 Gyr (Tegmark & Bostrom 2005)
- Mass extinctions: 5 major ones in 541 Myr (fewer than 1 per 100 Myr). Including 13 others gives 18, about 1 per 30 Myr. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

### Inferences
- Per-century equivalents (computed): GRB about 6×10⁻⁷ (0.00006%); supernova about 1×10⁻⁶ (0.0001%). Both are consistent with Ord's (unverified) "stellar explosion ~1 in 1 billion" existential figure, given that most such events would not be existential.
- For the website, these belong in an "other natural" bucket with a default slider value near zero.

### Gaps
- No modern updates (e.g., post-2020 GRB/supernova rate studies, or the 2020 Fields/Melott work on nearby supernovae) were retrieved.

## 9. Natural pandemics

### Takeaway
Natural pandemics are the natural risk most likely to cause mass death this century. XPT superforecasters gave a 1% chance of a natural-pathogen catastrophe (>10% of humanity dying) by 2100, but only 0.0018% for extinction. Ord put the existential risk at 1 in 10,000 per century. The fossil-record bound limits how high natural disease extinction risk can plausibly be, unless modern conditions amplify it.

### Cited Findings
- XPT superforecaster medians to 2100: natural pathogen catastrophe 1%, extinction 0.0018%; engineered pathogens 0.8% and 0.01%. — [FRI 19 Jul 2023](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Ord (2020): natural pathogens 0.01% (1 in 10,000) existential risk by 2120. — [FRI table](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT)
- Historical scale:
  - The Black Death may have killed a third of Europe, about 10% of the world population at the time.
  - The 1918 influenza killed an estimated 3–6% of the world population.
  - Medieval Europe survived losing 25–50% of its population without civilisational collapse.
  
  — [Wikipedia: Global catastrophic risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk)
- COVID: Ord notes it may have killed "more than 25 million people". Credible origin investigations are "about evenly split" between natural spillover and lab escape. — [Ord 2024](https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited)
- Upper-bound argument: "the claim that a natural disease poses a greater than 1 in 1,000 chance of extinction per year would require that anthropogenic conditions have increased the risk of natural disease by a factor of more than 14 to 870." Modern travel could turn a disease that was once only a local extinction risk into a global one (Manheim 2018). — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- Bill Gates (WSJ, 27 Jan 2025, via Newsweek and Wikipedia): 10–15% chance of a natural pandemic in the next four years, and 65–97.5% in the next 26 years. This is secondary and the definition of "pandemic" is unspecified. — [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)

### Inferences
- XPT superforecasters put roughly 1 in 550 natural-pathogen catastrophes going on to extinction (0.0018%/1%; computed). For ordinary people, the relevant outcome is the catastrophe tier: COVID-like or worse disruption with a ≥10% death toll. The extinction tier is negligible.
- A COVID-scale event (millions dead, multi-year disruption) is far more likely than either tier. On the Gates figure (unverified primary), it is close to a coin-flip or better over a few decades.

### Gaps
- No UK NRR 2025 pandemic likelihood was extracted. The 2023 NRR is widely reported to put a pandemic in its highest-impact band, with 5–25% likelihood over 5 years; that is unverified here.
- No WHO/CEPI quantitative "Disease X" probabilities were retrieved.

## 10. Fossil-record upper bound on natural extinction risk (Snyder-Beattie, Ord & Bonsall 2019)

### Takeaway
Humanity has survived at least 200,000 years, so annual natural extinction risk is almost certainly below 1 in 14,000 (about 0.7% per century), and likely below 1 in 87,000 (about 0.11% per century). Using the whole *Homo* lineage, it is likely below 1 in 870,000 per year. The bound covers only risks that have stayed constant or fallen over human history, not new anthropogenic ones.

### Cited Findings
- "The probability that humanity goes extinct from natural causes in any given year is almost guaranteed to be less than one in 14,000, and likely to be less than one in 87,000… our entire genus *Homo* produces even tighter bounds, with an annual probability of natural extinction likely below one in 870,000." — [Snyder-Beattie, Ord & Bonsall, *Sci. Rep.* 9:11054 (30 Jul 2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- Bound details:

| Survival record | Relative likelihood 10⁻⁶ (near-certain) | Relative likelihood 0.1 (likely) |
|---|---|---|
| 200 kyr (*Homo sapiens*) | μ < 6.9×10⁻⁵ (1 in 14,000/yr) | μ < 1.2×10⁻⁵ (1 in 87,000/yr) |
| 315 kyr (Jebel Irhoud fossil) | <1 in 22,800–23,000/yr | <1 in 137,000/yr |
| 2 Myr (*Homo*) | <1 in 140,000/yr | <1 in 870,000/yr |

  — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- Observation-selection bias changes the bounds by less than a factor of 2 in plausible models. The 10⁻⁶ bound changes by only about 1.2× as θ→0. Only implausible "fixed-time" observerhood models break the bound. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- Cross-checks against other data:
  - Mammalian background extinction is about 0.023–1.8 per million species-years.
  - Median hominin species lifespan is about 620–970 kyr (*H. erectus* >1.7 Myr).
  - All catalogued natural hazards fall below the bound, except the Rougier 17 kyr rate for ≥10³ km³ eruptions, most of which are probably not extinction-level.
  
  — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)
- The paper concludes "anthropogenic extinction risks are likely greater than natural ones". Wikipedia restates the bound as "1 in 14,000 per year (equivalent to 1 in 140 per century, on average)". — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7); [Wikipedia: Human extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_extinction)
- The paper received an Author Correction (11 Dec 2019). — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

### Inferences
- Per-century (computed): 1/14,000/yr ≈ 0.71%; 1/87,000/yr ≈ 0.115%; 1/870,000/yr ≈ 0.0115%. These are *ceilings*. The sum of individually estimated natural hazards (Ord ≈0.01%; XPT superforecasters ≈0.0055% per century) sits near or below the optimistic bound.
- Website use: a hard cap on the "total natural" slider at 0.7% per century, with a recommended default around 0.01%.

### Gaps
- None material; this is a primary source that was fully retrieved.
