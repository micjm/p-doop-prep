# Expert probability estimates and threat models for non-AI anthropogenic catastrophic/existential risks (nuclear, bio, climate, other)

Research compiled Oct 2026. Definitions used throughout unless noted:
- **XPT "catastrophe"** = a cause kills >10% of humans alive within a 5-year period; **XPT "extinction"** = global population falls below 5,000. Horizon "by 2100" from a 2022 baseline (~78 years). XPT forecasts were made June–Oct 2022 and published July 2023 (revised Aug 2023).
- **Ord "existential catastrophe"** = extinction OR unrecoverable collapse OR unrecoverable dystopia, over 2021–2120.
- Annualised conversions in the Inferences sections are my own, assuming a constant, independent annual hazard (a simplification that 80,000 Hours and Rodriguez also flag).

## Q1. Nuclear war (incl. nuclear winter): probabilities, Doomsday Clock, arsenals, nuclear-winter science, target regions

### Takeaway
Estimates of *some* nuclear use cluster around ~0.3–1% per year (≈20–50%+ by 2100), but estimates of nuclear *catastrophe* (>10% dead) by 2100 are ~4% (superforecasters) to ~8% (experts), and nuclear *extinction* by 2100 is ~0.07% (superforecasters) to ~0.55% (experts); 80,000 Hours puts existential risk at ~0.01%. The death toll in a large war is dominated by nuclear-winter famine (Xia et al. 2022: >5 billion for a US–Russia war), but the magnitude of nuclear winter is genuinely contested (Robock/Toon vs. LANL/Reisner).

### Cited Findings

**Probability of nuclear war / use (dated)**
- Luisa Rodriguez (Rethink Priorities, June 2019) aggregated historical, expert-survey and forecaster evidence: "about a 1.1% chance of nuclear war each year" and "the chances of a nuclear war between the US and Russia, in particular, are around 0.38% per year" (she cautions these are "deceptively precise"). — [Rodriguez/RP 2019](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/PAYa6on5gJKwAywrF/how-likely-is-a-nuclear-exchange-between-the-us-and-russia)
- Inputs in Rodriguez 2019: naive base rate (1 wartime use in 74 years) ≈1.4%/yr; Barrett, Baum & Hostetler (2013) median annual probability of *inadvertent* US–Russia nuclear war ≈0.9% (90% CI 0.02%–7%); 2008 Global Catastrophic Risks survey implies ≈0.39%/yr for nuclear war killing ≥1M, ≈0.12%/yr for ≥1B, ≈0.011%/yr for nuclear extinction; Lugar Survey (2005) median 20% nuclear attack within 10 yrs (≈2.21%/yr); PS21 Great Power Conflict Report (2015) median 4.72% US–Russia nuclear exchange in 20 yrs (≈0.24%/yr); Good Judgment Inc. superforecasters (2018) 1% chance of a state nuclear attack causing ≥1 fatality before 2021 (≈0.40%/yr) and ≈0.01%/yr for US–Russia specifically. — [Rodriguez/RP 2019](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/PAYa6on5gJKwAywrF/how-likely-is-a-nuclear-exchange-between-the-us-and-russia)
- Martin Hellman: a commenter (David Denkenberger) on the Rodriguez post cites "M.E. - 80,000 Hours table of all estimates since 2000 (annualised / implied by 2100): XPT superforecasters, nuclear weapon kills >1,000 people: 0.54%/yr (33.34% by 2100); XPT domain experts: 0.61%/yr (37.09%); Lugar 2005: 2.21%/yr (81.5%); Johns Hopkins APL (2021) "nuclear war": 1.00%/yr (53.18%); Good Judgment Inc (2018): 0.40%/yr (26.11%); **Metaculus (2024) nuclear detonation as an act of war (by 2050): 0.92%/yr (50.24%)**; **Metaculus (2024) global thermonuclear war by 2070: 0.28%/yr (19.11%)**; GCR survey 2008, war killing ≥1M: 0.39%/yr (25.55%). 80k's summary: "the typical annual risk of some kind of nuclear war is around 0.25% to 2.5%" (with >1%/yr "a little too high"), i.e. "20% to 80% chance of nuclear war this century." 80k's own scale estimate: "chance of a nuclear war in the next 100 years is something like 20–50%." (Profile published June 2024, last modified Oct 16, 2026.) — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)

Israel's use, if any, expected against Iran. — [XPT report, pp. 47–48](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)

**Catastrophe and extinction from nuclear war**
- XPT final medians, nuclear catastrophe (>10% dead): superforecasters 0.5% (2030), 1.83% (2050), **4% (2100)**; domain experts 1% (2030), 3.4% (2050), **8% (2100)**. Nuclear extinction: superforecasters 0.001% (2030), 0.01% (2050), **0.074% (2100)**; domain experts 0.02%, 0.12%, **0.55%**. (N=88 supers, N=13 nuclear experts.) Non-domain experts: 8% catastrophe / 0.19% extinction; general x-risk experts 7.24% / 0.7%; public survey 10% / 2%. — [XPT report, pp. 27, 45](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- XPT: "Nuclear weapons are seen as the most likely cause of a catastrophic event; AI is seen as the most likely source of human extinction." Forecasters judged a regional India–Pakistan-scale exchange would *not* by itself cross the >10% threshold, and saw "Nuclear winter … as the only plausible mechanism for human extinction due to nuclear war." — [XPT report, pp. 15, 48](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Prior existential/extinction estimates compiled by XPT (Table 14): Aird 2021 extrapolated median 1.58% (nuclear contribution to x-risk by 2100); Sandberg & Bostrom 2008 GCR survey 1% (extinction from all nuclear wars by 2100); Hilton & McIntyre (80k) 2022: 0.005–1% (by 2122); **Ord 2020: 0.1% (1 in 1,000) existential catastrophe via nuclear war by 2120**; Pamlin & Armstrong (Global Challenges Foundation) 2015: 0.005% "infinite impact" by 2115. — [XPT report, p. 50](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- 80,000 Hours additional estimates: Denkenberger (2018) 0.30%/yr existential catastrophe; Sandberg (2018) 0.10%/yr; Vasco Grilo (2024) human extinction 5.93×10⁻¹² per year (an extreme low-end, non-peer-reviewed estimate). 80k's own view: "existential risk from nuclear war is around 1 in 10,000" and "a 1 in 10 chance of over 10% of the population being killed as the result of a nuclear catastrophe by 2100," implying "something like a 1% chance you will die in a nuclear war." — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Luke Oman (co-author of Robock 2007) on the 150 Tg scenario: probability of global population reaching zero "in the range of 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 100,000." — quoted in [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Ord (July 2024 "Precipice Revisited"): "Nuclear risk is up" — Russian invasion of Ukraine brought nuclear powers "substantially closer to the brink," New START's expiry makes a new arms race "the most likely possibility," and the MacArthur Foundation (≈45% of field funding) withdrew, halving civil-society funding. He notes his original low rating partly reflected that "with our current arsenals there isn't a known scientific pathway for an all out war to lead to human extinction," but "now the arsenals are going to rise — perhaps a long way." — [Ord, The Precipice Revisited (2024)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/iKLLSYHvnhgcpoBxH/the-precipice-revisited)

**Doomsday Clock**
- Jan 27, 2026: set at **85 seconds to midnight**, "the closest the Clock has ever been to midnight in its history" (moved from 89 seconds). The SASB called for limits on nuclear arsenals, international AI guidelines, and multilateral agreements on biological threats. Prior settings: **2025 = 89 s**, 2023 = 90 s, 2020 = 100 s, 2018 = 2 min. The Clock has been reset 27 times since 1947; farthest was 17 minutes (1991). Climate has been factored in since 2007. — [Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, Doomsday Clock](https://thebulletin.org/doomsday-clock/)
- The Bulletin's site (Oct 2026) features headlines on a US–Israeli military campaign against Iran ("President Trump just started a dangerous, pointless war against Iran"); dates of these pieces were not captured. — [Bulletin homepage/Doomsday Clock page](https://thebulletin.org/doomsday-clock/)

**Current arsenals (FAS, early 2026)**
- ~12,187 warheads total held by nine states; ~9,745 in military stockpiles; ~3,912 deployed; ~2,100 US/Russian/UK/French warheads on high alert. US+Russia hold ~86% of the total inventory and 83% of stockpiled warheads. Military stockpiles are rising again (China, India, North Korea, Pakistan, UK, possibly Russia) even as total inventory falls via dismantlement. — [FAS Status of World Nuclear Forces 2026](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/)
- Country table (deployed strategic / stockpile / total): Russia 1,796 / 4,400 / 5,420; US 1,670 (+100 nonstrategic in Europe) / 3,700 / 5,042; France 280 / 290 / 370; China 34 / 620 / 620; UK 120 / 225 / 225; Israel 0 / 90 / 90; Pakistan 0 / 170 / 170; India 0 / 190 / 190; North Korea 0 / 60 / 60. US B61s deployed at 6–7 bases in Belgium, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Turkey and possibly the UK. US DoD projected China would have ~1,000 operational warheads by 2030. France announced in early 2026 it would stop disclosing arsenal figures; US/Russia stopped New START data exchanges in 2023 and the treaty has expired. — [FAS 2026](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/)
- Internal inconsistency: the same FAS page also says the inventory fell "to an estimated 12,321 at the beginning of 2026" (vs 12,187 in the table). Peak was ~70,300 in 1986. — [FAS 2026](https://fas.org/initiative/status-world-nuclear-forces/)
- 80k: if New START collapsed, US and Russian deployed warheads "could approximately double in size in the course of a few months" by uploading existing warheads (citing FAS). New START expired Feb 5, 2026. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)

**Nuclear winter science**
- Xia, Robock et al. (Nature Food, Aug 15, 2022): six scenarios of 5–150 Tg stratospheric soot, each a one-week war. "More than 2 billion people could die from nuclear war between India and Pakistan, and more than 5 billion could die from a war between the United States and Russia." India–Pakistan war could inject 5–47 Tg; US/allies–Russia >150 Tg. — [Xia et al. 2022, Nature Food](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0)
- Xia et al. mechanisms/timeline: climate impacts "last for about a decade but would peak in the first few years"; max cooling over crop regions 1.5 °C (5 Tg) to 14.8 °C (150 Tg) (Last Glacial Maximum was ~5 °C cooler); global crop calories −7% in years 1–5 even at 5 Tg (exceeding any anomaly in FAO records since 1961), up to −50% at 47 Tg, and ~−90% 3–4 years after a 150 Tg war. Under 150 Tg, "most countries would have less than 25% of the population survive by the end of Year 2." Assumes international food trade halts; stored food consumed in Year 1. — [Xia et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0)
- Xia et al. regional pattern: strongest losses at mid–high Northern Hemisphere latitudes; in the 27 Tg case, nuclear states at mid–high latitudes (China, Russia, US, France, North Korea, UK) see 30–86% calorie reductions while lower-latitude India, Pakistan, Israel see <10% (from climate effects alone, excluding local war damage). Australia (wheat-dominated, spring wheat may even benefit) and New Zealand fare best, likely receiving refugees; "African and Middle Eastern countries would be severely affected" by trade cut-offs. Unmodelled: radioactive contamination, UV/ozone loss, fuel/fertilizer/infrastructure loss, and adaptation via alternative foods. — [Xia et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0)
- Critique (Reisner et al., LANL, 2018): for the same 100×15 kt India–Pakistan scenario, Robock/Toon models show ~1–2 °C cooling lasting 3–10 years, whereas Reisner et al. find 0–0.5 °C with effects mostly limited to polar regions; the groups exchanged rebuttals (Robock et al. 2019; Reisner et al. 2019). Of nine published cooling studies compiled by Christian Ruhl, six include Robock or Toon. 80k's judgment: the right answer is "somewhere between," i.e. ~0 to −2 °C for 100×15 kt, and "up to around two billion people might die" from famine in that scenario, with a much lower median. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/); [Reisner et al. 2018, JGR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2017JD027331)
- Rodriguez's survey of smoke–famine studies (as summarised by 80k): cooling >1 °C "would plausibly kill around one billion people," and ~4 °C "around eight billion," highly uncertain. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Southern-hemisphere survival: nuclear winter models suggest much of the southern hemisphere would survive even a very large war; a study of New Zealand found food production would likely exceed survival calorie needs. Rodriguez's research suggests humanity is fairly likely to survive even if 99.9% die. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)

**What it looks like for ordinary people (direct effects)**
- One 800 kt warhead (most common Russian ICBM yield) over New York: lethal radiation within ~2.5 km; third-degree burns to ~10 km line-of-sight; near-total deaths within ~2.5 km from blast and most residential buildings collapsed within 6.5 km; NUKEMAP estimates up to ~2,000,000 fatalities. Ground bursts produce local fallout "with lethal effects lasting days or weeks" (unsheltered people in 100 rad/hr contours likely die). High-altitude bursts can produce EMP damaging electronics/power systems ~100–1,000 km away. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Direct blasts alone cannot kill everyone: destroying all urban areas would need ~175,000 800 kt weapons (~140,000 Mt) vs a historical peak of ~15,800 Mt. Knock-on effects include infrastructure loss, disruption of water/food/energy, healthcare and transport. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Targeting: US 2022 Nuclear Posture Review says the US does not intend to target civilian populations, but military/industrial targets in cities remain; no comparable Russian/Chinese statements found by 80k. More city targeting → more soot → greater nuclear-winter risk. — [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)

### Inferences
- Slider-ready ranges (per century): any nuclear use ≈20–50% (80k), nuclear catastrophe >10% dead ≈4–10% (XPT supers 4%, XPT experts 8%, 80k ~10%), nuclear extinction ≈0.07–0.55% (XPT), existential catastrophe ≈0.01% (80k) to 0.1% (Ord 2020; Ord says risk has risen since).
- Annualised: XPT superforecaster 4% catastrophe by 2100 ≈ 0.05%/yr; expert 8% ≈ 0.11%/yr. XPT nuclear extinction 0.074% ≈ 0.001%/yr; 0.55% ≈ 0.007%/yr.
- Most likely hot spots (inferred from XPT actor rankings + FAS deployments): NATO Europe/Russia (Ukraine escalation; US B61 host states), Korean Peninsula/Japan, Taiwan Strait/East Asia, South Asia (India–Pakistan), Middle East (Israel–Iran). In a full US–Russia war, Xia et al. imply famine deaths would fall worldwide, worst in mid/high-latitude Northern Hemisphere and trade-dependent Africa/Middle East; Australia/NZ/southern South America least affected.
- Timeline for a large war: blast/fire deaths in days; fallout deaths over days–weeks; global cooling peaking in years 1–2; famine mortality concentrated in Year 2 onwards after stored food is exhausted, lasting ~a decade.
- 2022–2026 developments (Ukraine war, New START expiry Feb 2026, China's buildup, French opacity, Doomsday Clock at a record 85 s, reported US–Israel strikes on Iran) all point upward relative to the 2022 XPT numbers, which pre-date them.

### Gaps
- Exact Xia et al. 2022 Table 1 per-scenario numbers (direct deaths and famine deaths per Tg scenario) were not extractable from the fetched page; only the abstract-level ">2 billion" and ">5 billion" were verified.
- Current (late-2026) Metaculus community predictions were not retrieved; the 0.92%/yr and 0.28%/yr figures are 80k's 2024 snapshots.
- Hellman's ~1%/yr estimate is cited only second-hand. Full 2026 Doomsday Clock statement text not retrieved.
- No authoritative public source on actual target lists; "most likely target regions" is inference.
- I did not find a newer formal FRI re-forecast of nuclear risk post-2022 (FRI published "Assessing Near-Term Accuracy in the XPT," Sep 2025, but its contents were not reviewed).

## Q2. Biological risks: engineered and natural pandemics, AI-bio uplift, mirror life

### Takeaway
Engineered-pandemic existential risk is rated far higher by EA-adjacent experts (Ord: 1 in 30 per century; XPT biosecurity experts: 1% extinction by 2100) than by superforecasters (0.01% extinction). For catastrophe (>10% dead) by 2100: supers ~0.8% engineered + 1% natural, experts ~3% engineered + 0.85% natural. Natural pandemics follow a fat-tailed distribution: a Spanish-flu-scale event has ~1/400 annual probability today, possibly tripling in coming decades. New concerns since 2022: AI uplift for bioweapons (2026 International AI Safety Report) and mirror bacteria (Science, Dec 2024).

### Cited Findings

**Expert/forecaster probabilities**
- XPT (asked in intake/postmortem surveys only, without rationale exchange, due to information-hazard concerns). Final (post-XPT) medians by 2100: **engineered-pathogen catastrophe: superforecasters 0.8%, domain experts 3%** (non-domain experts 2.5%, general x-risk experts 5%); **natural-pathogen catastrophe: supers 1%, experts 0.85%** (non-domain 1.5%, x-risk 1.9%); **engineered-pathogen extinction: supers 0.01%, experts 1%** (non-domain 0.1%, x-risk 1%); **natural-pathogen extinction: supers 0.0018%, experts 0.01%**. Pre-XPT values: engineered catastrophe 1.15% (supers) / 2.45% (experts); engineered extinction 0.1% / 0.7%; natural extinction 0.01% / 0.1%. Superforecasters "became less concerned … over the course of the tournament, but the predictions from biosecurity experts remained stable." — [XPT report, pp. 16–17, 62, 65](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- XPT combined "Biological" (superforecaster medians, 2100): 1.8% catastrophe; 0.012% extinction. — [FRI, "What do XPT forecasts tell us about AI risk?" (EA Forum, 2023), cross-risk table](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT/what-do-xpt-forecasts-tell-us-about-biological-risk)
- XPT pathogen killing >1% of humanity within 5 years — genetically engineered: supers 0.25% (2030), 1.5% (2050), 4% (2100); experts 1.22%, 8%, 10.25%. Non-engineered: supers 0.5%, 1.69%, 3.63%; experts 1%, 5%, 8.14%. FRI notes historical base rates (smallpox, Black Death, 1918 flu) suggest roughly 4–9% by 2050 and 11–23% by 2100. — [XPT report, pp. 62–66](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- XPT bioweapons by 2050: bioweapon use causing ≥1,000 deaths — supers 1 (state) + 1 (non-state) expected events; experts 1.4 (state) + 2 (non-state). Bioweapon causing ≥100,000 deaths — experts median 0, supers 0.15 (state) / 0.038 (non-state). Lab-escaped pathogen killing ≥1,000: <1 expected event by 2050 (supers 0.68, experts 0.5). Countries with bioweapons programs by 2050: experts 10.5, supers 7; Russia and North Korea most likely. WHO PHEICs killing ≥10,000 by 2050: supers 7, experts 5. — [XPT report, pp. 63–69](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Toby Ord, The Precipice (2020): "1 in 30 chance of existential catastrophe from 'engineered pandemics' by 2120" (as quoted by FRI). FRI's cross-risk table lists Ord's 2120 estimates as 3.3% engineered pathogens and 0.01% natural pathogens. — [XPT report, p. 63 fn. 120](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf); [FRI XPT summary table](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT/what-do-xpt-forecasts-tell-us-about-biological-risk)
- 2008 GCR survey (as quoted by FRI): experts estimated a 30% chance that the "single biggest engineered pandemic" would kill 1 million people by 2100. — [XPT report, p. 63 fn. 120](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Resource allocation: XPT experts' largest non-AI allocation in every resource question was "Engineered pathogen" (e.g. 22–27% of a hypothetical $10,000); superforecasters' largest non-AI allocation was usually Nuclear. — [XPT report, Table 11](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)

**Natural pandemic frequency**
- Marani et al. (PNAS, Aug 2021): global dataset of epidemics 1600–present; intensity (deaths / global population / duration) follows a generalized Pareto distribution with a slowly decaying power-law tail (exponent −0.71). Annual probability of a Spanish-flu-intensity event has ranged 0.27–1.9% since 1600; its **mean recurrence time today is 400 years (95% CI 332–489)**. "Extreme epidemics are relatively likely," and with rising zoonotic emergence "the yearly probability of occurrence of extreme epidemics can increase up to threefold in the coming decades." (An erratum was published Mar 2023.) — [Marani et al. 2021, PNAS (Europe PMC record)](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC8536331)
- Snyder-Beattie, Ord & Bonsall (Sci. Rep. 2019): humanity's 200,000-year survival implies the annual natural extinction rate (all natural causes) is <1 in 87,000 (modest confidence) and <1 in 14,000 (near certainty); a claim that natural disease poses >1/1,000 annual extinction risk would require anthropogenic conditions (e.g. travel) to have raised it 14–870×. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

**AI-bio uplift**
- International AI Safety Report 2026: general-purpose AI "can provide detailed information relevant to developing biological and chemical weapons," e.g. generating instructions and troubleshooting; "one study found that a recent model outperformed 94% of domain experts at troubleshooting virology lab protocols" (OpenAI o3 on the Virology Capabilities Test); "Multiple AI companies chose to release new models in 2025 with additional safeguards after pre-deployment testing could not rule out the possibility that they could meaningfully help novices develop such weapons." It stresses "substantial uncertainty … about how these capabilities affect risk in practice, given material barriers." — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/)
- Ord (2024): COVID showed weaknesses in defences; he states credible investigations of COVID origins were "about evenly split" on lab leak vs zoonosis (commenters dispute "evenly split"); he also noted that pandemic-preparedness spending after COVID was "disappointingly weak in the UK and even worse in the US," though vaccines were developed unprecedentedly fast and protective technologies (metagenomic sequencing, far-UVC, air quality) progressed. — [Ord, The Precipice Revisited (2024)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/iKLLSYHvnhgcpoBxH/the-precipice-revisited)

**Mirror life**
- Adamala et al., "Confronting risks of mirror life," Science 386:1351–1353 (Dec 12, 2024), 38 authors incl. Church, Endy, Esvelt, Inglesby, Lenski, Relman, Szostak, Venter, Winter. — [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/39666824)
- Accompanying Stanford technical report: in mirror bacteria all chiral molecules are reversed; they "could not evolve from existing life, but their creation will become increasingly feasible as science advances." Because immune defenses and predation rely on chiral interactions, "it therefore appears plausible, even likely, that sufficiently robust mirror bacteria could spread through the environment unchecked by natural biological controls and act as dangerous opportunistic pathogens in an unprecedentedly wide range of other multicellular organisms, including humans" — with risks to animals, plants and ecosystems. (Version 2 of the report published July 7, 2025.) — [Technical Report on Mirror Bacteria, Stanford Digital Repository](https://purl.stanford.edu/cv716pj4036)

### Inferences
- Slider-ready (per century, ~2022–2100): engineered pandemic catastrophe 0.8% (supers) – 3% (experts) – 5% (general x-risk experts); natural pandemic catastrophe ~1%; engineered extinction 0.01% (supers) – 1% (experts); Ord existential 3.3%. Annualised: 3% by 2100 ≈ 0.04%/yr; Ord 1/30 per century ≈ 0.034%/yr.
- Using Marani's 1/400 per year, the chance of at least one Spanish-flu-intensity pandemic in an ~80-year lifetime ≈ 18%; with a threefold rise (≈0.75%/yr) ≈ 45%.
- Bio is the area of *widest relative disagreement* after AI: on extinction, experts are ~100× higher than superforecasters (1% vs 0.01%); on natural-pathogen catastrophe, supers are slightly *higher* than experts.
- Casualty scenario for ordinary people (from mechanisms above): a fast-spreading respiratory agent affects all regions within weeks–months via air travel; main harms are direct mortality plus health-system collapse and supply-chain/worker absenteeism; mirror bacteria would be unusual in threatening crops, livestock and ecosystems simultaneously, with conventional immunity/antibiotic options potentially ineffective (inference from the report's framing).
- XPT bio numbers pre-date the 2025 frontier-AI bio-safeguard decisions; post-2025 AI uplift arguably pushes risk upward relative to 2022 estimates.

### Gaps
- No probability estimates were found for mirror-life catastrophe specifically; the report gives qualitative feasibility/risk judgments (often summarised elsewhere as a decade-plus timeline, but I did not verify a timeline in a fetched source).
- No quantified post-2024 expert survey of AI-enabled bio catastrophe probability was retrieved (e.g., FRI's later biorisk/AI studies, RAND or CLTR work).
- The EA Forum URL I used for the FRI cross-risk table redirected to the FRI AI-risk post; the table itself is FRI-authored, but readers may need the XPT PDF for verification.

## Q3. Climate change incl. tail warming: probabilities, existential vs catastrophic framing

### Takeaway
Mainstream projections now centre on ~2.1–3.9 °C by 2100 (median forecasts ~2.6 °C), with IPCC AR6 narrowing climate sensitivity (likely 2.5–4 °C; ~95% <5 °C) — which Ord argues lowers existential risk. Tail warming >6–9 °C requires both high emissions and high sensitivity; 80,000 Hours puts direct climate extinction below 1 in 1,000,000 and total (indirect) contribution ~1 in 10,000, whereas Kemp et al.'s "Climate Endgame" argues catastrophic/collapse scenarios are "dangerously underexplored" and should not be dismissed. XPT did not ask climate catastrophe/extinction questions because experts considered them too slow-moving and extremely unlikely.

### Cited Findings
- Kemp et al., "Climate Endgame" (PNAS, Aug 1, 2022; CSER/PIK/Exeter et al.): "Could anthropogenic climate change result in worldwide societal collapse or even eventual human extinction? At present, this is a dangerously underexplored topic." Proposes research on mass-extinction potential, mechanisms of human mass mortality, risk cascades (conflict, political instability, financial risk), and an "integrated catastrophe assessment." — [Kemp et al. 2022, PNAS](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC9407216)
- Kemp et al. on likelihood: current trajectory → 2.1–3.9 °C by 2100; full 2030 NDCs → 2.4 °C (1.9–3.0 °C); all long-term pledges → 2.1 °C (1.7–2.6 °C). IPCC ECS best estimate 3 °C, likely 2.5–4 °C, "there remains an 18% probability that ECS could be greater than 4.5 °C," distribution "heavy tailed." Temperatures >2 °C have not been sustained since before the Pleistocene (>2.6 Myr). Stratocumulus loss at high CO₂ could add "∼8 °C global warming." They claim RCP8.5/SSP5-8.5 "most closely matches cumulative emissions to date." — [Kemp et al. 2022](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC9407216)
- Kemp et al. on people: under SSP3-7.0 emissions and population, "by 2070, around 2 billion people are expected to live in" areas with >29 °C mean annual temperature (currently 0.8% of land; ~30 million people, mainly Sahara and Gulf Coast). Literature and IPCC focus on 1.5–2 °C; ≥3 °C is "underrepresented relative to their likelihood." Solar geoengineering carries "termination shock" tail risk. — [Kemp et al. 2022](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC9407216)
- Ord (July 2024): "It is now clear that we are not at all on RCP 8.5, and that our own path is headed somewhere between the lower two paths"; IPCC AR6 narrowed likely ECS from 1.5–4.5 °C to 2.5–4 °C and says it is "95% likely that the climate sensitivity is less than 5°"; these "reduc[e] the chance of extreme outcomes, which in turn reduces the existential risk." In comments he conceded "'mixed' might be a better summary of Climate" after being shown AR6's lowered impact thresholds ("reasons for concern" embers) and record recent heat. — [Ord, The Precipice Revisited (2024)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/iKLLSYHvnhgcpoBxH/the-precipice-revisited); contradicts Kemp et al.'s RCP8.5 framing in [Kemp et al. 2022](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC9407216)
- XPT (2022) global surface temperature vs 1850–1900, medians: superforecasters 1.47 °C (2030), 2 °C (2050), 2.6 °C (2100); experts 1.4 °C, 2 °C, 2.55 °C — closest to SSP2-4.5 but with wider ranges. XPT did not ask about climate catastrophe/extinction "because the impacts would be too slow-moving to meet our 'catastrophic' threshold … and in pilot interviews, climate experts told us they would place an extremely low probability on extinction risk from climate change." — [XPT report, p. 59](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- 80,000 Hours (climate profile, modified Oct 16, 2026): current policies → "around 2–3°C of warming by 2100" (IPCC current-policy projections 2.5–3.5 °C). Burning all recoverable fossil fuels (~3,000 GtC; ~2,000 ppm CO₂) gives "a 1 in 6 chance we'd see greater than 9ºC of warming by 2100"; with a cloud-feedback tipping point (~1,300 ppm), ~13 °C (possibly ~15 °C). Direct extinction: "less than a 1 in 1,000,000 chance"; indirect contribution to other existential risks "something like 1 in 10,000." — [80,000 Hours climate profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/climate-change/)
- 80k thresholds for habitability (citing Sherwood & Huber 2010, PNAS): wet-bulb temperatures above 35 °C would begin to occur with ~7 °C global warming; with 11–12 °C, such regions would cover "the majority of the human population as currently distributed." Sea level: ~1 m by 2100 at 5 °C (up to 2 m worst case); >15 m by 2300 "cannot be ruled out" under the highest emissions; 1 m without adaptation would displace ~0.5 billion people. Agriculture: >10 °C would likely destroy agriculture in India-like climates; up to 183 million additional undernourished and up to 4 billion facing chronic water scarcity by 2050 without major cuts (IPCC AR6 WGII FAQ). One expert's guess: 13 °C → hundreds of millions of deaths, not extinction. Species: worst-case models 15–37% of species "committed to extinction" by 2050 (Thomas et al. 2004). — [80,000 Hours climate profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/climate-change/)
- Weitzman (2009), as cited by Snyder-Beattie et al.: a "ballpark" 1% probability of ~20 °C of anthropogenic warming, which would make much of the planet uninhabitable via heat stress. — [Snyder-Beattie et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7)

### Inferences
- Slider-ready: probability of >4 °C by 2100 now appears low-but-nonzero under current policies (Kemp's current-trajectory range tops out at 3.9 °C; XPT medians 2.6 °C); >6 °C requires high-emission reversal plus high ECS (≈18% chance ECS >4.5 °C per Kemp/IPCC; ≈5% chance >5 °C per Ord/IPCC). Existential climate risk: ~1 in 1,000,000 direct to ~1 in 10,000 including indirect (80k) vs Ord's ~1 in 1,000 (2020, unverified here—see Gaps) and the qualitative "non-negligible, understudied" stance of Kemp et al.
- Catastrophe framing: climate is more plausibly a slow "risk multiplier" (famine, migration, conflict, state fragility over decades) than a 5-year >10%-mortality event; this is why XPT excluded it, and why slider design should probably use a longer window or a "contributing factor" toggle.
- Ordinary-people scenario: hardest hit are the tropics and subtropics (South Asia, Sahel, Middle East, Gulf), low-lying deltas and coastal megacities (South/Southeast/East Asia), and import-dependent poor countries; impacts unfold over decades with intensifying heatwaves, crop failures, water stress, displacement.
- Disagreement to flag: Kemp et al. say RCP8.5 matches cumulative emissions to date; Ord says we are clearly not on RCP8.5 (the two statements refer to different things—historical cumulative emissions vs. projected future pathway—but present opposite impressions).

### Gaps
- Ord's Precipice Table 6.1 climate value (widely reported as ~1 in 1,000 existential risk per century, with "other environmental damage" also ~1 in 1,000) could not be verified against the book or a primary reproduction in this session.
- IPCC AR6 per-scenario probabilities of exceeding 4 °C / 5 °C / 6 °C by 2100 were not extracted directly (only via 80k and Kemp). No 2025–2026 updated warming-probability literature (e.g., Climate Action Tracker 2025/2026, UNEP Emissions Gap 2025) was retrieved.
- No quantified expert estimate of "climate-triggered >10% mortality" was found.

## Q4. Other anthropogenic risks: Ord's "unforeseen/other anthropogenic," great-power war, totalitarianism, nanotech, ecological collapse, cyber

### Takeaway
Great-power war is the most probable of these: ~1 in 3 chance of a war between great powers (≥1,000 battle deaths/yr) by 2050 per 80k, with existential-level damage 0.05–2% this century. Stable global totalitarianism is estimated at ~0.3% (80k) to 5% over 1,000 years (Caplan). Ecological collapse is judged very unlikely to be existential (Kareiva & Carranza). Quantified estimates for nanotech and cyber-catastrophe were not found in primary sources.

### Cited Findings
- 80,000 Hours great-power conflict profile: "about a one-in-three chance that a new great power war breaks out in roughly the next 30 years" (specifically ~30% chance of a war with ≥1,000 battle deaths/yr involving at least one of US, China, Russia, India on each side before 2050); "reasonable to put the probability of such a conflict in the coming decades somewhere between 10% and 50%." Historical rate ~2 great-power wars per century (Levy; Goldstein; Braumoeller) → naive 2%/yr, ~80% by 2100. "Chance of an extinction-level war at between 0.025% and 1%"; "a war killing 800 million people is probably around three times more likely than a war killing eight billion"; overall chance war "seriously damages the long-term future this century is between 0.05% and 2%." — [80,000 Hours great power conflict](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/great-power-conflict/)
- Metaculus (as of 8 June 2023, via 80k): 17% US–China war (≥1,000 battle deaths) before 2035; 20% US–Russia war before 2050; 68% chance of ≥100 deaths in China–Taiwan conflict by 2050. — [80,000 Hours great power conflict](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/great-power-conflict/)
- Ord (2024): the Ukraine invasion made great-power war, which he had called "almost unthinkable … this decade" in 2020, "markedly worse than anyone anticipated." — [Ord, The Precipice Revisited (2024)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/iKLLSYHvnhgcpoBxH/the-precipice-revisited)
- 80,000 Hours stable totalitarianism: back-of-envelope over ~next century — 25% technology (esp. AI) makes entrenchment possible × 25% someone tries × 5% they succeed = **~0.3% (about 1 in 330)**. Bryan Caplan (2008): 5% that "a world totalitarian government will emerge during the next one thousand years and last for a thousand years or more" (0.1% if number of countries doesn't decrease; 25% if it falls to one). — [80,000 Hours stable totalitarianism](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/risks-of-stable-totalitarianism/)
- Ecological collapse: Kareiva & Carranza (CSER, Futures 2018) concluded biodiversity loss/ecosystem collapse is very unlikely to be an existential risk ("Extinctions and biodiversity loss are ethical and spiritual losses, but perhaps not an existential risk"); losing all pollinators would cut agricultural production by roughly 3–10%. — quoted in [80,000 Hours climate profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/climate-change/)
- Cyber: the 2026 International AI Safety Report says "more evidence has emerged of AI systems being used in real-world cyberattacks," including by state-associated groups. 80k notes cyberattacks on nuclear command systems are "highly plausible" and the entanglement of conventional and nuclear C3 systems could raise accidental-war risk. — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/); [80,000 Hours nuclear profile](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
- Natural baseline for comparison: XPT non-anthropogenic catastrophe by 2100 — supers 0.05%, experts 0.09%; non-anthropogenic extinction — supers 0.0043%, experts 0.004% (essentially agreement). Ord's non-anthropogenic existential risk (2120): 0.01%. — [XPT report, pp. 16–17](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf); [FRI XPT summary table](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT/what-do-xpt-forecasts-tell-us-about-biological-risk)
- Ord total existential risk (2120): 1 in 6 (16.67%); unaligned AI 1 in 10. — [Wikipedia, The Precipice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precipice:_Existential_Risk_and_the_Future_of_Humanity); [FRI XPT summary table](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/K2xQrrXn5ZSgtntuT/what-do-xpt-forecasts-tell-us-about-biological-risk)

### Inferences
- Great-power war functions as a *risk factor* multiplying nuclear, bio and AI risks; a slider could treat it as an upstream probability (≈30% by 2050) with conditional escalation probabilities.
- Ordinary-people scenario for great-power war short of nuclear use: regional (Taiwan Strait/East Asia, Eastern Europe), with global effects via semiconductor and shipping disruption, sanctions, energy/food price shocks; cyberattacks on grids and communications plausible as an opening move (inference).
- Totalitarian lock-in is mostly an AI-enabled pathway in current analyses, so it overlaps with AI-risk sliders handled elsewhere.

### Gaps
- Ord's Precipice values for "unforeseen anthropogenic risks" (widely reported as ~1 in 30), "other anthropogenic risks" (~1 in 50; covering e.g. dystopia/totalitarianism and nanotech), and "other environmental damage" (~1 in 1,000) could not be verified against a primary source this session — treat as unverified.
- No primary quantitative estimates found for nanotechnology catastrophe (the 2008 GCR survey reportedly includes molecular nanotech weapons, but that report was not fetched).
- No quantified expert probability for a cyberattack causing catastrophic (>10% dead) infrastructure collapse was found.
- Global Challenges Foundation annual reports and CSER publications were not reviewed beyond Kemp et al.

## Q5. Superforecaster vs domain-expert disagreements (XPT) for each risk

### Takeaway
Across risks, XPT domain experts gave higher probabilities than superforecasters, with the gap largest for AI and engineered pathogens (extinction: 100× for engineered bio, ~8× for AI and nuclear) and smallest for nuclear catastrophe (2×) and natural/non-anthropogenic risks (near parity). Total: experts 20% catastrophe / 6% extinction by 2100 vs superforecasters 9.05% / 1%. Minds rarely changed during the tournament.

### Cited Findings
- XPT totals by 2100 (median, 95% CI): total catastrophic risk — superforecasters 9.05% [6.13–10.25], experts 20% [15.44–27.60], non-domain experts 28.95%, general x-risk experts 11.56%; total extinction — superforecasters 1% [0.55–1.23], experts 6% [3.41–10.00], non-domain 6.6%, general x-risk 5%. Public survey of 912 college graduates: 5% extinction. — [XPT report, pp. 5, 16–17](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Per-risk comparison (supers vs domain experts, by 2100): AI catastrophe 2.13% vs 12%, AI extinction 0.38% vs 3%; nuclear catastrophe 4% vs 8%, nuclear extinction 0.074% vs 0.55%; engineered-pathogen catastrophe 0.8% vs 3%, extinction 0.01% vs 1%; natural-pathogen catastrophe 1% vs 0.85%, extinction 0.0018% vs 0.01%; non-anthropogenic catastrophe 0.05% vs 0.09%, extinction 0.0043% vs 0.004%. "On nuclear catastrophe, experts and superforecasters reached similar conclusions. That's less true of nuclear extinction risk—but it is even less true with regard to AI." — [XPT report, pp. 16–17, 27](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Sample/composition caveats: 80 experts (32 AI, 15 general x-risk, 12 bio, 12 nuclear, 9 climate) and 89 superforecasters; ~42% of experts vs 9% of superforecasters had attended an EA meetup; expert dropout 51% vs 19% for superforecasters (per a commenter). Nuclear-specific expert medians are from N=13. — [FRI announcement & discussion](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Findings on process: "Few minds were changed during the XPT"; "Predictions about risk were highly correlated across topics"; forecasters with higher "intersubjective accuracy" (better at predicting others' views) "estimated lower probabilities of catastrophic and extinction risks from all sources." — [FRI announcement](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)
- Elicitation sensitivity: the same public sample gave a median 5% extinction by 2100 when asked for a percentage but "1 in 15 million" when asked for "1-in-X" odds with low-probability reference examples — a ~6-orders-of-magnitude framing effect (not tested on experts/supers). — [FRI announcement & discussion](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a); [XPT report, p. 30](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- Superforecasters' arguments for lower nuclear risk: nuclear weapons are hard to acquire/use, retaliation and stigma, cheaper alternatives (cyber), long-run pacification; higher-risk arguments: Ukraine, Taiwan, proliferation, New START expiry. Base-rate approaches varied (e.g., a 2/(5n) zero-event formula gives 0.51% by 2100 for catastrophe). — [XPT report, pp. 49–50](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/635693acf15a3e2a14a56a4a/t/64f0a7838ccbf43b6b5ee40c/1693493128111/XPT.pdf)
- The XPT was later published as "Subjective-probability forecasts of existential risk: Initial results from a hybrid persuasion-forecasting tournament" (International Journal of Forecasting, 2025) and FRI released "Assessing Near-Term Accuracy in the XPT" (Sep 2, 2025). — [FRI XPT page](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt); [Unjournal comment on FRI announcement](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/un42vaZgyX7ch2kaj/announcing-forecasting-existential-risks-evidence-from-a)

### Inferences
- Suggested slider anchors (by 2100; "low" = XPT superforecasters, "high" = XPT domain experts, "Ord" = existential by 2120):

| Risk | Catastrophe >10% dead (low / high) | Extinction (low / high) | Ord 2020 existential | Other anchors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nuclear war | 4% / 8% | 0.074% / 0.55% | 0.1% (says higher in 2024) | 80k: ~10% catastrophe, ~0.01% x-risk |
| Engineered pandemic | 0.8% / 3% | 0.01% / 1% | 3.3% (1 in 30) | x-risk experts 5% catastrophe |
| Natural pandemic | 1% / 0.85% | 0.0018% / 0.01% | 0.01% | Marani: 1918-scale ≈0.25%/yr |
| Climate | not asked | not asked | ~0.1% (unverified) | 80k: <0.0001% direct, ~0.01% indirect |
| Great-power war | — | 80k: 0.025–1% extinction-level | — | 80k: ~30% GP war by 2050 |
| Totalitarian lock-in | — | — | (in "other anthropogenic", unverified) | 80k 0.3%; Caplan 5%/1,000 yrs |
| Non-anthropogenic | 0.05% / 0.09% | 0.0043% / 0.004% | 0.01% | Snyder-Beattie: <1/87,000 per yr |
| **Total (all causes incl. AI)** | 9.05% / 20% | 1% / 6% | 16.7% (1 in 6) | public 5% extinction |

- Because XPT forecasts were frozen in Oct 2022 (before ChatGPT, before New START's expiry, before the 2025 AI-bio safeguard decisions and the 2026 record Doomsday Clock), sliders should note that both nuclear and bio estimates plausibly drift upward from these anchors.
- The largest uncertainty for a "prior" slider may be elicitation method rather than group: the 6-OOM framing effect in the public survey suggests offering both percentage and "1 in X" displays.

### Gaps
- XPT catastrophe/extinction by 2030 and 2050 for bio and for combined totals by expert group were not all extracted (only nuclear by year, and superforecaster totals: 0.85%/3.85%/9.05% catastrophe and 0.01%/0.3%/1% extinction for 2030/2050/2100 via the FRI summary table).
- No XPT-style superforecaster vs expert comparison exists for climate catastrophe, great-power war, totalitarianism, nanotech or cyber.
- Results of FRI's 2025 near-term accuracy assessment (which group was more accurate on 2024 questions) were not reviewed.
