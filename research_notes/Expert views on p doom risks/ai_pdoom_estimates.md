## Q1: What p(doom) values have named experts and lab leaders stated publicly?

### Takeaway
Named-individual estimates span ~8 orders of magnitude: from ~0% (LeCun <0.01%, Andreessen 0%) through a "lab leader" cluster around 10–25% (Amodei 25%, Musk 10–30%, Hinton 10–20% all-things-considered, Bengio 20%) to >90% (Tegmark, Leahy, Yudkowsky, Yampolskiy). Most numbers lack an explicit timeframe or definition, and many people give different numbers on different occasions.

### Inferences
- Rough clusters for slider presets: (a) dismissive/accelerationist ≈0–0.01% (LeCun, Andreessen, Booch); (b) skeptical-but-nonzero ≈1–5% (Aaronson, MacAskill low end, Silver, journalists); (c) frontier-lab/"godfather" mainstream ≈10–25% (Amodei, Musk, Hinton all-things-considered, Bengio, Khan, Buterin, Ord, Hoffman); (d) safety-researcher concerned ≈30–50% (Christiano, Lifland, Alexander, Meindertsma, Mostaque, Irving-conditional); (e) high-doom ≈70–99%+ (Kokotajlo, Zvi, Krueger, Critch, Duvenaud, Hendrycks, Leahy, Tegmark, Yudkowsky, Yampolskiy).
- The CEOs of OpenAI and Google DeepMind have not given numbers (as of these sources). Only Amodei among frontier-lab CEOs has publicly given one (25%).
- Several high-doom estimates (Hendrycks, Zvi, Kokotajlo, Buterin) appear to have risen between 2023 and 2025. This is based on a small number of paired data points.
- Many recent figures come from one interviewer's podcast (Liron Shapira's "Doom Debates"), which asks everyone the p(doom) question. That selection could over-represent people willing to state a number.

## Q2: What do systematic surveys and forecasting exercises show?

### Takeaway
Among ML researchers (AI Impacts 2023, n=2,778), the median is **5%** and the mean **~14–16%** for AI causing human extinction or severe permanent disempowerment. If the cause is framed as loss of control, the figures rise to a median of **10%** and a mean of **~19%**. Superforecasters in the 2022 XPT gave only **0.38%** for AI extinction by 2100, against **3%** from domain experts (12% for AI catastrophe). Metaculus sits in between and has trended upward.

### Cited Findings

**AI Impacts Expert Survey on Progress in AI (ESPAI) — Grace et al., "Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI" (arXiv 2401.02843, v2 Apr 2024)**
- Sample: 2,778 researchers who published in 2022/23 at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, IJCAI, JMLR. Fielded fall 2023. About 4× the 2022 survey, which covered NeurIPS+ICML only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Results by question wording ([arXiv Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2)):

| Question wording | 2022: N; mean; median | 2023: N; mean; median |
|---|---|---|
| "future AI advances causing human extinction or similarly permanent and severe disempowerment of the human species" | 149; 15.7%; 5% | 1,321; **16.2%**; **5%** (IQR 19%) |
| "human inability to control future advanced AI systems causing human extinction or similarly permanent and severe disempowerment" | 162; 20.5%; 10% | 661; **19.4%**; **10%** (IQR 29%) |
| same as Q1 but "within the next 100 years" | not asked | 655; **14.4%**; **5%** |
| "Extremely bad (e.g. human extinction)" share of long-run HLMI impact (conditional on HLMI existing) | mean 14% | n≈2,704; mean **9%**, median **5%** |

- Depending on wording, 41.2%–51.4% of respondents gave ≥10% to extinction/severe disempowerment. 38% gave ≥10% to "extremely bad" outcomes of HLMI. 57.8% gave ≥5% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- One in ten respondents put ≥25% on outcomes in the range of human extinction. 48.4% of net optimists still gave ≥5% to extremely bad outcomes — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- The share giving ≥10% to "extremely bad" fell from 48% (2022) to 38% (2023). The mean fell from 14% to 9% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Respondents who had thought more about AI risk gave higher numbers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- 70% said AI safety research should be prioritized more — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- PauseAI summarises the ML researcher means as "9–19.4% (median 5–10%) depending on question design" — [PauseAI](https://pauseai.info/pdoom).
- Vox/Wikipedia headline figure: the "within 100 years" item, mean 14.4% and median 5% — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)), citing [Vox](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2024/1/10/24032987/ai-impacts-survey-artificial-intelligence-chatgpt-openai-existential-risk-superintelligence) and the [AI Impacts wiki](https://wiki.aiimpacts.org/ai_timelines/predictions_of_human-level_ai_timelines/ai_timeline_surveys/2023_expert_survey_on_progress_in_ai).

**Forecasting Research Institute — Existential Risk Persuasion Tournament (XPT)**
- Held in 2022 (before ChatGPT). 80 experts and 89 superforecasters (169 total) over a multi-stage tournament. Working paper published 10 Jul 2023, revised 8 Aug 2023 — [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt).
- Definitions: "catastrophe" means an event killing >10% of humanity within five years. "Extinction" means population reduced below 5,000 — [ACX summary](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- AI-caused extinction by 2100: superforecasters median **0.38%**; domain experts **3%**; AI catastrophe from domain experts **12%** — [PauseAI citing XPT PDF](https://pauseai.info/pdoom); also "0.4% ... compared to the domain experts' 3%" — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- Superforecasters' total global catastrophe (all causes) by 2100: 9%. Metaculus was ~30% in summer 2022 and 38% by Jul 2023 — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- Conditional: the median superforecaster's AI-extinction-by-2100 forecast rose from 0.38% to **1%** conditional on AGI by 2070 (FRI postmortem survey) — [ACX quoting FRI](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- "We document large-scale disagreement and minimal convergence of beliefs ... with the largest disagreement about risks from artificial intelligence" — [FRI abstract](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt).
- Peer-reviewed version: Karger et al., *International Journal of Forecasting* (Jan 2025). "Specialists were more pessimistic, especially on long-run threats posed by AI... neither side materially moved the other's views"; "strong AI-risk proponents made particularly extreme long- but not short-range forecasts and over-estimated the long-range AI-risk forecasts of others" — [FRI/IJF](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/subjective-probability-forecasts-of-existential-risk-initial-results-from-a-hybrid-persuasion-forecasting-tournament).
- FRI later published "Assessing Near-Term Accuracy in the XPT" (2 Sep 2025) — [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt). Contents not fetched.

**Metaculus (community forecasts as of fetch, Oct 2026)**
- "If an AI catastrophe occurs, will it reduce the human population by ≥95%?": **25%** (343 forecasters) — [Metaculus Q2513](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/2513/if-an-ai-catastrophe-happens-will-it-cause-near-extinction/).
- "By 2100, will the human population decrease by ≥10% during any 5-year period?" (all causes): **40%** (431 forecasters) — [Metaculus Q1493](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/1493/global-population-decline-10-by-2100/).
- Historical: Metaculus AI catastrophe risk rose from 6% (summer 2022) to 15% (Jul 2023) — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).

**Other**
- 2021 survey of 44 AI safety researchers: mean ~30% — [AF archive via PauseAI](https://web.archive.org/web/20221013014859/https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/QvwSr5LsxyDeaPK5s/existential-risk-from-ai-survey-results) [OLD].

### Inferences

## Q3: How do definitions and timeframes differ?

### Takeaway
"Doom" variously means literal extinction (XPT: <5,000 survivors), "extinction or similarly permanent and severe disempowerment" (AI Impacts), "catastrophe" killing >10% within 5 years (XPT), "AI takeover" (Christiano), or vague "really, really badly" (Amodei). Timeframes range from "within 10 years" to "by 2100" to unspecified or conditional on ASI. Even when the respondents are the same, a change of wording moves results 2× or more.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia: "The exact outcomes in question differ from one prediction to another"; criticism centres on ambiguity of scenario, "the time frame that the probability covers, and the conditions that are assumed" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)); see also [Isaac King, "Stop talking about p(doom)", LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EwyviSHWrQcvicsry/stop-talking-about-p-doom).
- PauseAI: "not everyone is using the same definition... Most notably the time horizon is often not specified" — [PauseAI](https://pauseai.info/pdoom).
- Christiano's decomposition, as an example of how much definitions matter ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xWMqsvHapP3nwdSW8/my-views-on-doom)):
  - AI takeover is 22% in total: 15% from AI that humans build, plus 7% from later successor AI.
  - "Most humans die within 10 years of building powerful AI" is 20%: 11% via takeover and 9% via non-takeover causes such as war or terrorism.
  - "Irreversibly messed up our future within 10 years of building powerful AI" is 46%. That is 22% takeover, plus 9% extra extinction, plus 15% other (dystopia, unwise commitments).
  - He separates "extinction risk" from "existential risk": "there's a good chance of bad futures without extinction".
- AI Impacts wording effects: "human inability to control" gives a median of 10% versus 5% for generic "AI advances". Adding "within the next 100 years" lowers the mean from 16.2% to 14.4% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Extreme framing sensitivity: Karger et al. 2023 found college graduates gave answers "nearly six orders of magnitude apart" on AI x-risk depending on elicitation format; example low-probability reference events lowered estimates — [arXiv (Grace et al. §5.2.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- XPT: "catastrophe" means >10% of humans dead within 5 years; "extinction" means <5,000 humans — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- Conditional definitions: Irving's 50% is conditional on ASI being created. Hubinger's >10% covers "within the next decade". The 15.ai founder's <1% is "extinct by 2100 due to AI" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)).
- Hinton's own split: 10–20% "all-things-considered" versus >50% "independent impression" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)).
- Scott Aaronson: ~2% for the paperclip-maximizer-style scenario, but a "much higher probability for an existential catastrophe in which AI is causally involved" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)).

### Inferences
- For website sliders, separate at least three quantities: P(extinction), P(permanent disempowerment/takeover), and P(catastrophe >10% deaths). Also make the horizon explicit (e.g., by 2100) and offer a "conditional on AGI/ASI" toggle. Otherwise named-expert presets aren't comparable.
- Rough ordering when quoting the same person or group: catastrophe > existential (incl. disempowerment) > extinction. XPT experts give 12% vs 3%, and Christiano gives 46% vs 20%.

### Gaps
- No standardized cross-person dataset exists that normalizes definitions. Normalization would have to be manual and judgment-based.

## Q4: What is the spread between groups, and why (XPT and adversarial collaboration findings)?

### Takeaway
On AI extinction by 2100, domain experts are about 8× superforecasters (3% vs 0.38%). ML researchers (median 5%) and especially AI-safety researchers (~30% mean) are higher still. FRI's adversarial collaborations found that neither side moved after structured debate. The gap mostly reflects differing beliefs about how powerful AI will be this century and whether benchmark progress reflects general intelligence. The gap shrinks over very long horizons: both groups put 30–40% on extreme negative outcomes by 2500 and beyond.

### Cited Findings
- XPT: superforecasters 0.38% vs domain experts 3% for AI extinction by 2100. For AI catastrophe, experts give 12% — [PauseAI](https://pauseai.info/pdoom); [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- FRI's open puzzle: "why were superforecasters so unmoved by experts' much higher estimates of AI extinction risk, and why were experts so unmoved by the superforecasters' lower estimates?" — [FRI](https://forecastingresearch.org/xpt).
- Adversarial collaboration (Rosenberg, Karger et al., *Risk Analysis*, 3 Apr 2025) ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/belief-updating-in-ai-risk-debates-exploring-the-limits-of-adversarial-collaboration)):
  - "AI experts saw greater threats from AI than did expert forecasters, and neither group changed its long-term risk estimates."
  - The two groups preregistered "cruxes whose resolution by 2030 would sway their views".
  - "These persistent differences shrank as questioning moved across centuries, from 2100 to 2500 and beyond, by which time both groups put the risk of extreme negative outcomes from AI at **30%–40%**."
- IJF paper: disagreement is consistent with "boundedly rational agents searching for confirmatory evidence as the risks of embarrassing accuracy feedback receded" — [FRI/IJF](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/subjective-probability-forecasts-of-existential-risk-initial-results-from-a-hybrid-persuasion-forecasting-tournament).
- FRI quote (via ACX): superforecasters' lower risk "isn't primarily driven by an expectation of another 'AI winter'". There was "little or no separation between the groups on the three questions about 2030 performance on AI benchmarks". Disagreement is "over whether measures of progress represent optimization for narrow tasks, versus symptoms of more general intelligence" — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- XPT participant Peter McCluskey reported no one with "substantial expertise in machine learning" among the experts he met. He said superforecasters suspected AI progress was hype, and that attention was spread thin over 59 questions — [ACX quoting LessWrong](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- ACX's view: most XPT forecasters already expected AGI by 2100. The main disagreement was whether it would cause catastrophe once built — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- On short-term XPT questions, experts looked better than superforecasters:
  - Superforecasters' median for 2024 max training cost was $35M, below GPT-4's reported ~$63M.
  - Turing-test date: superforecasters said 2060, experts 2045.
  - [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament)
- ML researchers vs safety researchers: the AI Impacts median is 5–10%. The 2021 safety-researcher survey mean is ~30% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2); [PauseAI](https://pauseai.info/pdoom).

### Inferences
- An ordering for presets, from low to high: superforecasters (~0.4%) < XPT domain experts (~3%) < median ML researcher (~5%) < mean ML researcher (~15%) < lab leaders (~10–25%) < safety researchers (~30%) < MIRI-style (>90%).
- The XPT predates ChatGPT, so its numbers may be stale. Metaculus AI-catastrophe forecasts rose ~2.5× between mid-2022 and mid-2023.

### Gaps
- The adversarial-collaboration paper's specific numbers for 2100 (per group, after collaboration) weren't retrieved. Only the abstract was fetched.
- I didn't find a newer post-2023 superforecaster-vs-expert p(doom) re-elicitation. LEAP may contain one but wasn't verified.

## Q5: What AGI timelines come from the same sources?

### Takeaway
ML researchers (AI Impacts 2023) put a 50% chance on HLMI (all tasks) by **2047**, 13 years earlier than in 2022, and 50% on full automation of labor by **2116**. The Metaculus community currently forecasts the first "general AI" announcement around **Dec 2030** (80% interval roughly Apr 2028 – Dec 2036).

### Cited Findings
- AI Impacts 2023, HLMI: 10% by **2027**, 50% by **2047** (n=1,714). The 2022 survey gave 2060 for 50%, and 2016 gave 2061. The 10% date moved from 2029 to 2027 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- AI Impacts 2023, full automation of labor: 10% by 2037, 50% by **2116**. The 2022 survey gave 2164 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Framing effect: the 50% HLMI horizon was **17 years** under fixed-probability framing versus **34 years** under fixed-years framing. Respondents with undergraduate education in Asia expected HLMI 11 years earlier — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Intelligence explosion (2023 medians): 20% for explosive progress 2 years after HLMI and 80% for 30 years after. AI is "vastly better than humans" at 10% 2 years after HLMI and 60% 30 years after. These have been stable since 2016 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02843v2).
- Metaculus "When will the first general AI system be devised, tested, and publicly announced?" stands at **01 Dec 2030** (range 05 Apr 2028 – Dec 2036; ~1.9k forecasters) as of Oct 2026 — [Metaculus Q5121](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/5121/date-of-artificial-general-intelligence/).
- XPT: forecasters broadly agreed AGI (Bostrom-resolved) was likely by 2100, with experts somewhat more confident. Turing-test medians were 2060 (superforecasters) and 2045 (experts) — [ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/the-extinction-tournament).
- Bengio (2023): 50% probability of human-level AI within a decade, which is an input to his ~20% p(doom) — [Wikipedia note citing ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)).
- Kokotajlo's 70–80% figure comes from his "2027 Intelligence Explosion" talk, which implies short timelines centered near 2027 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom)). The exact median wasn't verified.

### Inferences
- Timeline defaults:
  - ML-researcher median: ~2047 (HLMI).
  - Forecasting-community median: ~2030 (Metaculus).
  - Short-timelines camp: ~2027–2028 (Kokotajlo/AI 2027).
  - The 2023 ESPAI is now ~3 years old, and its numbers have been shortening quickly (13 years per year in 2022→2023). Treat 2047 as an upper-ish anchor.
- Higher stated p(doom) tends to go with shorter timelines (Kokotajlo, Bengio), though this is anecdotal.

### Gaps
- The XPT median AGI years for each group weren't retrieved (the PDF was too large).
- Lab-leader AGI timelines weren't collected this session: Amodei ("powerful AI" ~2026–27), Altman, Hassabis (~5–10 years), Legg (50% by 2028). These are well-known claims but I didn't verify them here.
- No 2024–2026 ESPAI timeline update found.
