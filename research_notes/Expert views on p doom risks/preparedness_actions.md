# Preparedness Actions for Global Catastrophic Risks: What Experts Recommend Individuals and Households Do, Mapped per Risk

Research date: 21 Oct 2026. Default reader: an ordinary household in the US; other countries noted where sources allowed. Source dates are given inline where available. Method note: the search tooling was limited (one search engine started blocking automated queries partway through), so a few key official sources (the Swedish "If Crisis or War Comes" brochure, CDC potassium-iodide guidance, NZ Get Ready) could not be retrieved. ## Q1. Pandemic prep: what do biosecurity experts and community guides recommend (respirators/P100, stockpiling, far-UVC, filtration)?

### Takeaway
The expert-backed core of personal pandemic prep is a **pre-bought, NIOSH-approved respirator setup (an elastomeric half-mask with P100 filters, or a stock of N95s) plus eye protection, gloves and disinfectant**. A guide published in 2025 puts this at about **$120–180 per person**. Add enough savings and supplies to stay home for a long time. Far-UVC is promising, but in 2026 it is still a research and pilot technology, not a mature consumer recommendation. Biosecurity organisations (Blueprint Biosecurity, Johns Hopkins CHS) mainly push *reusable respirator stockpiling* at the institutional level. That matches what households can do themselves.

### Cited Findings
- EA Forum "Personal Pandemic Preparedness" (Alvin Ånestrand, 30 Jul 2025) recommends a per-person PPE set costing **~$120–180 total**. The author says plainly that he is not an expert. — [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/TZHxp99Ag2WpE7Ety/personal-pandemic-preparedness)
  - Respiratory: **NIOSH-approved only**. N95 = at least 95% filtration; P100 = at least 99.97% (the post says "99.8%"; NIOSH's P100 standard is 99.97%, see Gaps). Two options: (a) **20–30 N95s**, the cheapest choice with less protection; or (b) **a reusable P100 respirator with 20–30 filter replacements**, the highest protection and possibly cheaper over time. Full-face models give the most protection. Check approval numbers on the NIOSH Certified Equipment List. — same source
  - Eye protection: **ANSI/ISEA Z87.1 sealed goggles**, indirect-vent and anti-fog, because "some airborne agents can infect you through your eyes." — same source
  - **50+ nitrile gloves**, 70–85% alcohol sanitiser (aim for ~80% ethanol or 75% isopropyl), and EPA List N surface disinfectants with the correct wet-contact time. — same source
  - Optional: AAMI level 4 gowns or EN 14126 class 5/6 coveralls, only for pathogens spread through body fluids. — same source
  - Non-equipment advice: have enough savings to cope if you can't work, plan for remote work and grocery delivery, and "imagine the lockdowns during the Covid pandemic, but potentially much worse." Practise with the equipment beforehand. — same source
  - The author's stated reason: AI is increasing biosecurity risk ("AIs providing expertise to bioterrorists"). — same source
- Related EA Forum posts listed alongside it include Jeff Kaufman's **"You Should Get a Reusable Mask"** (119 karma) and an H5N1 planning thread. This shows reusable elastomerics are a common community recommendation. Only titles were seen, not content. — [EA Forum (related-posts list)](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/TZHxp99Ag2WpE7Ety/personal-pandemic-preparedness)
- Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security (Jul 2024), *The Major Role of Reusable Respirators in Increasing Respiratory Protection*: demand for respiratory PPE in a future pandemic, "particularly early on, likely will far exceed both the supply in the Strategic National Stockpile (SNS)," and "a growing body of literature and recommendations support the need for greater use and stockpiling of RRs [reusable respirators] in the US." — [CHS PDF](https://centerforhealthsecurity.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/2024-07-reusablerespirators.pdf) (search-result snippet)
- CDC/NIOSH describe elastomeric half-mask respirators (EHMRs) as a way to ease disposable-N95 shortages, because they "can be cleaned, disinfected, and reused." — [CDC stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/124822/cdc_124822_DS1.pdf) (snippet)
- Clinical caveat: many hospitals required a surgical mask over the **exhalation valve** of elastomerics for source control during COVID, because of asymptomatic spread. — [PMC8185410](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8185410/) (snippet)
- **Blueprint Biosecurity** (the nonprofit behind the 2024 "Blueprint for Far-UVC") in 2026 works on four "pathogen-agnostic" programmes: far-UVC, PPE (reusable respirator stockpiles, "addressing barriers to effective use by critical workers," surge manufacturing), glycol vapors, and conventional germicidal UV (GUV). A Sept 2026 post is titled "A Respirator in a Warehouse Is Not the Same as Respiratory Preparedness." In other words, fit, training and having the respirator on hand matter, not just owning one. — [Blueprint Biosecurity](https://blueprintbiosecurity.org/)
- Status of far-UVC (Blueprint, 2026): "shows promise," but research is still under way on **safety** (long-term eye exposure, **ozone generation** and secondary chemistry), **real-world efficacy**, and **standards and regulation**. Blueprint has directed $4M+ to 23 research initiatives. Current pilots are in hospitals, sports arenas, offices, schools and farms, with no mention of homes. — [Blueprint far-UVC](https://blueprintbiosecurity.org/program/far-uvc/)
- Ready.gov's basic kit includes only a "dust mask (to help filter contaminated air)" plus soap, sanitiser and wipes, which is well below the biosecurity-community standard. — [Ready.gov Build a Kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit) (updated 07/01/2026)
- The Finnish 72-hours programme (SPEK, Finnish National Rescue Association, with the Ministry of the Interior) lists "infectious diseases: hygiene is key" as a module of home preparedness. — [72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/en/)
- The International AI Safety Report 2026 says general-purpose AI lets novices "access and contextualise" bio-relevant information faster. It cites a study in which OpenAI's o3 outperformed 94% of domain experts at troubleshooting virology lab protocols. It lists DNA synthesis screening as a societal resilience measure. — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/) (retrieved by another researcher; URL is the report's home page)

### Inferences
- **Cost tiers for the website** (derived from the $120–180/person figure and the item list above):
  - Tier 0 (~$0–30): hygiene supplies plus a pack of NIOSH N95s.
  - Tier 1 (~$120–180/person): elastomeric P100 half-mask, spare filters, sealed goggles, gloves, disinfectant.
  - Tier 2: a longer-term stay-home plan, i.e. food and water stock (see Q3) plus a cash buffer.
  - Effort: low (one afternoon of ordering, plus fit-checking and practice).
  - Scenarios helped: natural pandemics, engineered pandemics, wildfire smoke, nuclear/volcanic ash (a P100 also filters particulates). That makes respirators unusually robust across scenarios.
- Elastomerics need no resupply, so they are better than disposables for very long or severe (engineered) pandemics in which supply chains fail.
- Far-UVC and home HEPA filtration should go in a "promising / situational" bucket, not "core," until safety and efficacy evidence matures. This applies to far-UVC in particular. HEPA's evidence base was not retrieved this session.
- Blueprint's institutional framing (respirator plus training plus access) implies households should actually fit-test or at least seal-check their respirators and store them where they can reach them fast.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve Kevin Esvelt / SecureBio's specific personal recommendations (e.g., his "Delay, Detect, Defend" essay and his call for stockpiling P100 elastomerics for essential workers). Search was blocked.
- The 2024 "Blueprint for Far-UVC" document itself and any consumer cost figures for far-UVC lamps (e.g., ~$400–1,000+ per fixture is commonly cited) are unverified.
- Peer-reviewed studies on portable HEPA or Corsi-Rosenthal box filtration in homes (cost ~$60–100 for a DIY box) were not retrieved.
- The P100 efficiency figure: the EA Forum post says 99.8%, but NIOSH 42 CFR 84 defines P100 as ≥99.97%. I could not confirm this against a primary source in this session.
- I found no expert cost-effectiveness ranking of personal pandemic interventions.

## Q2. Nuclear war and nuclear winter: official guidance, location choice, island refuges, food

### Takeaway
Official US guidance (Ready.gov, last updated Jan 2025) is to **"Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned"**: after a detonation you have at least 10 minutes to reach a brick or concrete building or basement, and you should stay there at least 24 hours. This is free and saves lives in all but the blast zone. For the global-famine tail (nuclear winter), peer-reviewed work by Boyd & Wilson (Risk Analysis, 2023) finds **Australia, New Zealand, Iceland, the Solomon Islands and Vanuatu** the most resilient island nations. Even they face collapse risk from loss of trade (diesel, fertiliser, parts). 80,000 Hours judges nuclear-winter extinction "highly unlikely" but billions of deaths plausible. That makes household food storage and location relevant for *survival* rather than for preventing extinction.

### Cited Findings
- **Ready.gov "Radiation Emergencies"** (last updated 01/07/2025):
  - "The best way to stay safe in any radiation emergency is to get inside, stay inside and stay tuned." — [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/nuclear-explosion)
  - Prepare now: several ways to receive alerts (FEMA App, local alerts, **battery or hand-crank radio**); an emergency kit with a spare change of clothes per person, **a trash bag for contaminated clothing**, sealed food, water and medicine; a family communications plan with an out-of-state contact. — same
  - During: with warning, take cover; if outside, lie face down. "After a detonation, you will have **10 minutes or more** to find an adequate shelter before fallout arrives." The best shelters are multi-story buildings, basements, brick or concrete walls, underground garages and subways. "Remain in the most protective location... for the **first 24 hours**... Radiation levels decrease rapidly." Turn off fans and HVAC that draw in outside air; close windows and fireplace dampers. — same
  - After: "Removing your outer layer of clothing can remove **up to 90%** of radioactive material." Shower with soap and water; hand sanitiser doesn't help. Food from inside buildings and sealed containers (wiped off) is safe; don't eat garden produce until authorities say so. — same
- **80,000 Hours nuclear profile** (Benjamin Hilton, published Jun 2024, modified 16 Oct 2026):
  - Estimates a **20–50% chance of nuclear war in the next 100 years**, and puts existential risk from nuclear war at **~0.01%** (the literature ranges 0.005–1%). — [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/nuclear-security/)
  - "Nuclear winter models tend to suggest that humans in much of the southern hemisphere would survive." It also says nuclear-winter science is contested: Robock/Toon (pessimistic) versus Reisner et al./Los Alamos (optimistic). — same
  - Ground-burst fallout is "concentrated and local, with lethal effects lasting days or weeks". People inside the ~100 rads/hour contour "would likely be killed if they're not in some kind of shelter." A high-altitude burst could cause an **EMP** damaging electronics ~100–1,000 km away. — same
- **Boyd & Wilson, "Island refuges for surviving nuclear winter and other abrupt sunlight-reducing catastrophes," *Risk Analysis* 43(9):1824–1842 (2023)**: from a food self-sufficiency threshold analysis plus resilience indices, "**Australia, New Zealand, Iceland, the Solomon Islands, and Vanuatu** appear most resilient." However, NZ "is threatened in scenarios of no/low trade, has precarious aspects of its energy supply, and shortcomings in manufacturing of essential components... inadequate preparations and critical failures in these systems could see rapid societal breakdown." — [Europe PMC record, DOI 10.1111/risa.14072](https://doi.org/10.1111/risa.14072)
- **Wilson, Prickett & Boyd, *NZ Medical Journal* 136(1574) (Apr 2023)**: NZ food exports equal **3.9× current national dietary energy needs** (~8,150 kcal/person/day). Under nuclear-winter scenarios from minimal to severe, diverted exports would still supply **1.5–3.6×** needs. But agriculture "could be at risk of various levels of collapse from lack of imports (e.g., diesel, fertiliser, pesticides, seeds, and machinery parts) and from socio-economic collapse." Funded by the EA Long-Term Future Fund. — [DOI 10.26635/6965.6004](https://doi.org/10.26635/6965.6004)
- **ALLFED review (García Martínez, Behr, Pearce, Denkenberger, *Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr* 2025)**: "Global food storage is limited." Key levers are society-level: "Maintaining international trade and prioritizing crops for food instead of animal feed or biofuels is paramount." Methods range from mature (crop relocation, grass-fed ruminants, greenhouses, seaweed, fishing) to novel resilient foods. The review mainly recommends **government** national plans and task forces. — [DOI 10.1080/10408398.2024.2431207](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2024.2431207)
- Wikipedia (secondary) summarises Denkenberger et al. 2022 (*IJDRR* 73:102798) as finding "~98–99% confidence" that marginal work on resilient foods has higher impact than AGI safety. These are ALLFED-affiliated authors, so the result is contested and self-interested. It also notes that global food stockpiling has been proposed but "the monetary cost would be high." — [Wikipedia: Global catastrophic risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk); primary: [IJDRR](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2022.102798)
- Benjamin Todd (Dec 2024) suggests "making a plan to go somewhere outside of major cities with several months of supplies, where you can... wait out a pandemic, internet shutoff, or fallout cloud." — [Benjamin Todd Substack](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi Mowshowitz (Mar 2023), citing RAND staff who reportedly skipped retirement savings during the Cold War: should a small but real nuclear risk "radically change your actions? I think the answer here is a clear no," unless your actions are relevant to the risk. — [Zvi, AI: Practical Advice for the Worried](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)

### Inferences
- **Action tiers:**
  - (a) Free: memorise "get inside, stay inside, stay tuned," know the nearest basement or concrete core at home and work, enable the FEMA App and Wireless Emergency Alerts.
  - (b) Low cost (~$30–60): hand-crank/NOAA radio, trash bags, plastic sheeting and duct tape (already in the Ready.gov kit).
  - (c) Moderate: 2+ weeks of shelf-stable food and water, which covers fallout sheltering and also grid and supply shocks.
  - (d) High cost / high effort: living far from likely targets, or relocating or getting residency in the Southern Hemisphere (AU/NZ). This only matters if you put real weight on large-scale exchange and nuclear-winter scenarios.
- Island-refuge research points to a *national* resilience advantage, but the papers stress that this advantage depends on maintaining fuel, fertiliser and manufacturing. A website should not present NZ/AU as "safe." Present them as "best-placed if they prepare."
- Household food storage helps in the short term (weeks to months). Multi-year nuclear-winter famine is a society-level problem (ALLFED's focus), which an individual can address mainly by donating or advocating.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve the **potassium iodide (KI)** guidance (CDC/FDA). From general knowledge, it protects only the thyroid from radioactive iodine, should be taken only when officials instruct, and is mainly relevant near reactors or fallout. This is unverified this session.
- Target maps and "distance from likely targets" analyses (e.g., counterforce targets like ICBM fields in MT/ND/WY, SSBN bases) were not retrieved.
- The Xia et al. 2022 *Nature Food* figures (e.g., ~5 billion deaths in a 150 Tg US–Russia war) were seen only in passing in another researcher's material. I didn't fetch them.
- FEMA's "Nuclear Detonation Safety: Food, Drinking Water and Medicine" PDF (linked from Ready.gov) was not read.
- I found no EA Forum or LessWrong "nuclear war prep" post with specifics, because of the search block.

## Q3. Grid collapse, solar storms, cyberattacks, and general emergency kits (Ready.gov, Finland 72h, Sweden, NZ)

### Takeaway
Official guidance converges on **household self-sufficiency for several days**. The US says "several days," with 1 gallon of water per person per day. Finland's 72h programme says "at least three days" and adds staying warm in a cooling home and psychological resilience. The basics: water, food, a battery/hand-crank radio, lights, power banks, cash, medications, documents, a half-full gas tank, and a communications plan. These cheap, robust actions help in grid failure, solar storms, cyberattacks, natural disasters, the early phase of nuclear events, and pandemics.

### Cited Findings
- **Ready.gov Build a Kit** (updated 07/01/2026): "After an emergency, you may need to survive on your own for several days."
  - Basic kit: **water (one gallon per person per day for several days, for drinking and sanitation)**, several days of non-perishable food, battery/hand-crank radio plus NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert, flashlight, first aid kit, extra batteries, whistle, dust mask, plastic sheeting, scissors and duct tape (shelter-in-place), moist towelettes, garbage bags and ties, wrench or pliers to shut off utilities, manual can opener, local maps, cell phone with charger and backup battery. — [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/kit)
  - Additional: prescription medications ("About half of all Americans take a prescription medicine every day"), glasses, infant and pet supplies, **cash**, important documents (electronic or waterproof container), sleeping bags, change of clothes, fire extinguisher, matches, hygiene items. — same
  - Keep kits at home, at work (to shelter at least 24 hours) and in the car. Review your needs annually. — same
- **Ready.gov Space Weather** (updated 03/05/2026): space-weather power outages can cause loss of water and wastewater systems, refrigerated food and medications, HVAC, communications (including GPS and satellite), public transit, and **fuel distribution** ("Gas stations rely on electricity to power their pumps").
  - Prepare: build a kit, make a family communications plan, **keep the car's gas tank at least half full**, know the manual release on your garage door, keep extra batteries and chargers including a car charger. Refrigerated meds keep several hours in a closed fridge.
  - Afterwards: discard food held at ≥40°F for 2+ hours. — [Ready.gov Space Weather](https://www.ready.gov/space-weather)
- **Finland 72 hours (72tuntia.fi, SPEK with the Ministry of the Interior)**: "Households should prepare to cope independently for **at least three days**... store at least three days' worth of food supplies, water and medicines." People should know "where to get reliable information during a disruption and how to cope in a residence that is getting colder and colder."
  - Modules: power outage, home food supply, water ("You need containers or buckets to collect and store water"), sheltering indoors ("may require that you shut down ventilation and seal off windows"), **psychological resilience**, communication and reliable information, **cybersecurity**, infectious diseases.
  - The 2025 Home Preparedness brochure is linked. — [72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/en/)
- Benjamin Todd expects an intelligence explosion to destabilise the world order, "maybe leading to threats of nukes, bioweapons, cyberattacks... maybe through the 2030s." He recommends several months of supplies and a place to go outside major cities. — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- John-Clark Levin (LessWrong, Feb 2026): "**Back up irreplaceable data to cold storage**... storage drives not connected to the internet," because powerful AI raises the risk that a cyberattack destroys cloud or local data. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)
- The International AI Safety Report 2026 lists "strengthening critical infrastructure" and "incident response protocols (for AI-assisted cyberattacks)" as societal resilience measures. It says these efforts are "uneven and largely untested." — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/)

### Inferences
- **Durations differ across official sources.** US: "several days." Finland: at least 72 hours. In general knowledge, though not retrieved this session, Sweden's 2024 brochure says about one week, and NZ and some US expert guidance recommend 2 weeks. A website could offer a slider: 72h, then 1 week, then 2 weeks, then 1–3 months (Todd-style, for high-prior users).
- **Rough US costs** (my estimates; no source priced these):
  - Water: 14 gal/person for 2 weeks, ~$15–30 in jugs or ~$100–200 for barrels.
  - 2-week food stock: ~$100–200/person.
  - Radio: ~$30–60.
  - Power banks: ~$30–80.
  - Cash: none spent, just set aside.
  - Portable power station or solar: ~$300–2,000 and up.
  - Generator: ~$500–1,500 and up, with carbon-monoxide risk.
- Cold-storage data backup (~$50–100 for an external drive) is a cheap, robust action against cyber and AI-related data loss.
- "How to cope in a residence that is getting colder" (Finland) shows that **heating resilience** (warm bedding, safe indoor heat source) matters for winter grid failure. US guidance underweights this.

### Gaps
- The **Swedish "Om krisen eller kriget kommer / If Crisis or War Comes" (Nov 2024)** brochure could not be fetched (MSB English URL returned 404; Sweden's civil-protection agency may have been reorganised). Its specifics, e.g. water per person per day and the one-week framing, are unverified.
- I didn't retrieve NZ "Get Ready," UK "Prepare," Swiss emergency-supply guidance, or the Red Cross checklist.
- No source was retrieved on **ham/GMRS radio** (licensing cost: the FCC Technician licence fee is ~$35 and the GMRS licence ~$35 per general knowledge, unverified).
- No expert estimate was retrieved for the probability of a Carrington-class storm (e.g., ~1–2%/decade figures circulate) or for the duration of a large grid-down event.
- EMP-specific household guidance (Faraday storage, etc.) was not retrieved. It is also of contested value.

## Q4. Advanced AI risk: what AI-risk thinkers recommend individuals do (finance, careers, skills, community, mental health, relocation, advocacy), and skeptical views

### Takeaway
There is a fairly consistent "practical AGI prep" canon from Benjamin Todd (80,000 Hours co-founder), Zvi Mowshowitz and John-Clark Levin:
- **Stay informed and use frontier models.**
- **Keep saving and building assets.**
- Learn AI-complementary skills (AI use, social and management skills, physical trades).
- Be careful with leverage.
- Cut avoidable mortality risk (because future life-extension may raise the value of staying alive).
- Back up data, build community and mental health.
- Consider citizenship in AI-wealthy countries.
- Above all, **keep living a "normal life."**

These sources explicitly say personal prep **does nothing** in true extinction scenarios. Todd: "In that case, all you can do is try to enjoy your remaining years." The highest-leverage response for those scenarios is helping the transition go well (careers, donations, advocacy). Thinkers disagree on investing in AI companies, on spending versus saving, and on delaying children.

### Cited Findings
**Skeptical / framing views on whether personal prep matters**
- Todd (Dec 2024): "one possibility is we all die. In that case, all you can do is try to enjoy your remaining years... In many scenarios, the world will be so radically transformed that any efforts to prepare will be rendered moot... Focus on the scenarios where you can influence the outcome." He says making the transition go well for society "given the insane stakes, should be where we focus." Going "off grid" is "overrated as a way to protect yourself from AGI risk (as with going to Mars, it won't protect you)." — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi (Mar 2023): "**Normal Life is Worth Living**, even if you think P(doom) relatively soon is very high." He gives ten reasons, e.g., "A 'normal' future could still happen," and living like there's no tomorrow "rapidly loses its luster." He warns about community "information cascades" that led people to burn bridges, citing Cate Hall's admitted earlier over-reaction. "Burning yourself out, stressing yourself out, tying yourself up in existential angst all are not helpful. It would be better to keep yourself sane and healthy and financially intact, in case you are later offered leverage." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Zvi on the "no regrets" question: "Not that you shouldn't have done anyway... Keeping your situation flexible, and being mentally ready to change things." — same

**Finance**
- Todd: **save money**, because wages may rise and then collapse, and money keeps options open. "**Invest the money in things that do well in the lead up to AGI**"; "it seems possible to do a lot better than a standard 60:40 portfolio" (not financial advice). He links to Zvi, Carl Shulman and LessWrong posts on the topic. "If you have a safe way to take **long-dated fixed rate debt**, consider that it'll be cheap to pay back if there's been a lot of growth." — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi: still save for retirement, "Yes, to most (but not all) of the extent" you otherwise would. Think "build up asset value over time." In the US you can raid retirement accounts for ~10% penalty plus tax if needed. **Don't take on lots of debt planning not to repay it** ("No, except for a mortgage... Save your powder"). On buying a house: "Maybe." Starting a business: "Yes." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Zvi on investing in AI companies: "**Not if the company could plausibly be funding constrained**," because investment accelerates AI. Big Tech with unlimited cash is the exception. He declined to make an AI ETF. This **conflicts** with Todd's more permissive "invest in things that do well." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Levin (LessWrong, Feb 2026), in **conflict** with the "save" advice: "**Take the Italy trip**." He argues AGI means the future goes very well (abundance) or very badly (death), so "if you're in your 30s or younger, you'll usually be better off spending any dollar you make today than waiting to spend it after 2050." He adds that this doesn't mean maxing out credit cards. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)
- A LessWrong search snippet ("How to (hopefully ethically) make money off of AGI") argues: "if you expect both 1) AGI soon, and 2) many/most jobs replaced, then I think people shouldn't assume they'll be able to earn any income in future periods." — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/CTBta9i8sav7tjC2r/how-to-hopefully-ethically-make-money-off-of-agi) (snippet only)

**Career and skills (job displacement)**
- Todd: learn skills that rise in value before AGI: applying AI to real problems, personal effectiveness, social skills, learning how to learn, entrepreneurship and management, ML, robotics, hardware or cybersecurity, communications, getting things done in government, and **complex physical skills** (e.g., data-centre and robotics construction). — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi: "I wouldn't abandon your job just yet... If you stay ahead of the curve learning to use AI to improve your performance, that should also help a lot." On the margin, learn to code. — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Levin: "**Foster human relationships**". Jobs most resistant to automation "will largely revolve around human relationships." — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)
- Evidence base (International AI Safety Report 2026): there is "no effect on overall employment" so far, "but some signs of declining demand for early-career workers in some AI-exposed occupations." Evidence on retraining effectiveness is "mixed." — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/). The Forecasting Research Institute (Mar 2026), citing Brynjolfsson, Chandar et al. 2025, reports a **13% relative employment decline for ages 22–25** in the most AI-exposed occupations. Humlum & Vestergaard find the Danish decline uncorrelated with firm-level AI adoption. — [Forecasting Research Institute, "Forecasting the Economic Effects of AI"](https://forecastingresearch.org/) (retrieved by another researcher)

**Location / citizenship (power concentration and AI wealth)**
- Todd: "**Get citizenship of a country that'll have lots of AI wealth**". He says the US is "by far the best positioned," with allies (EU, especially the Netherlands/ASML, plus UK, Japan, South Korea, Australia, Canada) likely to share benefits. He also suggests a plan to leave major cities with several months of supplies. — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)

**Health, lifestyle, life planning**
- Levin: "**Minimize your lifestyle risks**". For Americans aged 15–44, "about 58% of mortality risk comes from three causes: accidents, suicide, and homicide" (citing CDC WISQARS 2023). Avoid motorcycles, drunk or distracted driving and hard drugs; attend to mental health. Reason: AGI medicine may greatly extend lifespans. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)
- Todd (comment): look after your health and avoid accidents, because "a tech explosion could bring life extension tech much earlier." He mentions cryonics as a consideration. — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Todd: "**Prioritise things you want to be in place before AGI**; delay anything you can delay three years." He says delaying children by ~3 years "could make sense" (he calls this controversial). — same. Levin: "Don't rush into having kids" (fertility medicine will improve). — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi). **Conflict:** Zvi says "Does It Still Make Sense to Try and Have Kids? Short Answer: Yes." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Levin: back up data to cold storage; "rethink privacy" (assume future AI can analyse today's recorded data); "wean your brain off quick dopamine"; "prioritize time with elders"; "grow in virtue." — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)

**Information, community, mental health**
- Todd: "**Seek out people who have some clue what's going on**". He names Carl Shulman, Paul Christiano, Ajeya Cotra, Daniel Kokotajlo, the 80,000 Hours Podcast, Dwarkesh, Zvi, Epoch AI. "Use the most advanced models." "In COVID, it was very helpful to be part of a group, so seek out people you could live with and who are willing to take non-common-sense actions." Invest in "mental health, groundedness, and ability to deal with stress over long periods... That might include trying to help." — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi: form your own model rather than deferring. When doom is talked about at home, "'you may not get to grow up' is not a helpful thing to say to... kids." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- Finland's 72h guide includes "psychological resilience" as a formal preparedness pillar. — [72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/en/)

**Contributing (AI)**
- Zvi warns: "the default outcome of those working in AI in order to help is to end up working primarily on capabilities, and making the situation worse." He calls working on capabilities or funding it "maximally bad". He considers jailbreak and red-team tinkering "generally a good thing." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- A LessWrong post, "Principles for the AGI Race," says: "consider the alternatives before deciding to join an AGI lab, or consider switching to civil society." — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/aRciQsjgErCf5Y7D9/principles-for-the-agi-race) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Robust AI-prep actions** (low regret whatever the timeline):
  - Learn and use frontier AI tools (free to ~$20–200/month).
  - Keep an emergency fund and keep saving.
  - Build social and relationship skills.
  - Cut accident and lifestyle risk (free).
  - Back up data offline (~$50–100).
  - Strengthen community ties and mental health.
- **Contested, prior-dependent actions:** shifting the portfolio toward AI/compute equities (ethics disputed, and the risk is concentrated); long-dated fixed-rate debt (mortgage-only per Zvi); spending more now (Levin) versus saving (Todd, Zvi); delaying children (Todd, Levin) versus not (Zvi); citizenship or relocation (high cost).
- For a website: personal prep for AI should be framed as covering the **"non-doom but turbulent" scenarios**: job displacement, power concentration, AI-enabled bio and cyber attacks. For **misalignment-extinction** scenarios, the only meaningful actions are risk-reduction contributions and psychological wellbeing.
- AI-enabled bio and cyber threats mean a high AI prior should also raise the weight on pandemic PPE (Q1) and grid/cyber kits (Q3).

### Gaps

- I didn't retrieve 80,000 Hours' AI problem profile or career guide content, or specific "skills AI makes valuable" pages, beyond Todd's summary.

## Q5. Climate extremes: location, insurance, heat preparedness

### Takeaway
Official guidance on climate extremes is mostly about **heat**. Ready.gov says extreme heat causes "the highest number of annual deaths among all weather-related hazards." Recommended actions: real cooling (not fans alone), know your local cooling centres, insulate and shade windows, check on vulnerable neighbours, and plan for heat-related power outages. Within EA, climate is generally seen as a lower *existential* risk than AI, bio or nuclear, so most climate prep is about local hazards (heat, flood, wildfire smoke, insurance) rather than catastrophe.

### Cited Findings
- **Ready.gov Extreme Heat** (updated 07/16/2026): extreme heat is temperatures "above 90 degrees for at least two to three days." It "is responsible for the highest number of annual deaths among all weather-related hazards." Older adults, children and the chronically ill are at higher risk. — [Ready.gov Heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat)
  - Before: "Do not rely on a fan as your primary cooling device... fans... do not reduce body temperature." Identify cooling places (libraries, malls). Cover windows, weather-strip, use reflective window film, insulate, ventilate the attic, **install window AC**. Low-income households can use **LIHEAP**. — same
  - During: cool showers, light clothing, hydrate, avoid midday exertion, check on neighbours. "Heat waves may cause you to lose power" (links to power-outage prep). — same
  - It lists heat stroke signs (above 103°F, hot dry skin, confusion): call 911 and cool the neck, armpits and groin. — same
- Levin argues individuals should "**not go overboard on environmentalism**" at personal cost (e.g., "Don't risk dehydration or heat stroke to avoid bottled water"), because AGI-era tools will mitigate climate more cheaply later. This is an opinion, and contested. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DHvymwaGXHwwrwZQP/eleven-practical-ways-to-prepare-for-agi)
- Zvi groups fatalism about climate as an "impending inevitable extinction event" with religious end-times thinking as a pattern that leads to poor decisions. — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- The Snyder-Beattie/Ord/Bonsall upper-bound paper (Sci Rep 2019) cites Weitzman's ballpark of a **~1% chance of 20°C of warming**, which would make much of the planet uninhabitable. It presents this as a tail scenario, not a forecast. — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7) (retrieved by another researcher)
- Boyd & Wilson's island-refuge criteria (food self-sufficiency, energy, manufacturing) also apply to "multiple breadbasket failure," a climate-linked scenario among their keywords. — [Risk Analysis 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/risa.14072)

### Inferences
- **Action tiers:**
  - Free: know heat-illness signs, locate cooling centres, set up a neighbour check-in plan.
  - Low: window shades or reflectors (~$20–100), a window AC unit (~$150–500).
  - Moderate: insulation, a backup power station to run a fan or medical devices during outages.
  - Wildfire smoke: the P100 or N95 from Q1, plus a HEPA purifier (cost unverified).
  - High: choosing where to live based on flood, fire and heat risk, and reviewing insurance (flood insurance is separate from homeowners policies in the US; general knowledge, unverified).
- Heat prep overlaps strongly with grid-failure prep (outages during heat waves), so cooling resilience is a robust action for warm-climate users.

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve "climate haven" research (e.g., studies ranking US cities such as the Great Lakes region, or First Street Foundation risk scores).
- I didn't retrieve sources on insurance-market retreat (e.g., insurers leaving California and Florida), the NFIP flood insurance programme, or catastrophe bonds.
- I didn't retrieve an expert view on household-level prep for catastrophic climate tails (e.g., Kemp et al. 2022 "Climate Endgame" appears only as a citation).
- I didn't retrieve wildfire and flood guidance (Ready.gov pages exist but weren't fetched).

## Q6. General societal collapse / supply-chain failure

### Takeaway
Experts in this space mostly treat long-duration collapse as a **society-level** resilience problem: trade, fuel, fertiliser, manufacturing and food redirection (ALLFED, Boyd & Wilson). For individuals the recommendations are modest:
- extend standard kits from days to weeks or months;
- join a trusted group or community;
- keep cash and savings;
- have a fallback location outside cities;
- learn practical skills.

Expert voices (Todd, Zvi) caution against "going off grid" or extreme bunker-style prepping as AGI-era or nuclear-era strategies.

### Cited Findings
- ALLFED: "Global food storage is limited." Interventions include crop relocation, grass-fed ruminants, greenhouses, seaweed, fishing and novel resilient foods. Governments "could implement national plans and task forces." — [Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/10408398.2024.2431207)
- Boyd & Wilson: even food-surplus islands face "rapid societal breakdown" if energy, manufacturing and trade fail. — [Risk Analysis 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/risa.14072)
- Wilson et al.: NZ agriculture depends on imported "diesel, fertiliser, pesticides, seeds, and machinery parts" and on the financial system continuing to function. — [NZMJ 2023](https://doi.org/10.26635/6965.6004)
- Wikipedia (secondary): "Some survivalists stock survival retreats with multiple-year food supplies". It notes Robin Hanson argues that a refuge permanently housing as few as 100 people would significantly improve humanity's survival odds. That is a civilisational strategy, not household advice. — [Wikipedia: Global catastrophic risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk)
- Historical resilience: "Medieval Europe survived the Black Death without suffering anything resembling a civilization collapse despite losing 25 to 50 percent of its population." — same (secondary)
- Todd: a plan to go "outside of major cities with several months of supplies," and to be "part of a group" willing to act early. — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi: "Keeping yourself healthy, cultivating good habits and remaining flexible and grounded are probably some good places to start." He says capital matters in some post-transition worlds. — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- The Cotton-Barratt, Daniel & Sandberg (2020) "defence in depth" framework divides risk work into **prevention, response, resilience**. Household prep sits in "resilience." — [Global Policy, PMC7228299](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7228299) (via Wikipedia)

### Inferences
- Extending stock from 2 weeks to 3+ months costs roughly $300–1,000 per person in bulk staples like rice and beans (my estimate, unsourced). It needs storage space and rotation. The value is highest under high priors on nuclear winter, severe pandemic or prolonged supply-chain failure.
- Community ties (neighbours, mutual-aid groups, CERT training) appear repeatedly in qualitative advice and cost little. They are a candidate "robust" action even though no quantified evidence was retrieved.

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve guidance on CERT (FEMA Community Emergency Response Team) training, mutual-aid networks, or skills curricula (first aid, food preservation).
- I found no study quantifying the survival benefit of household food stocks in prolonged collapse.
- Long-term food storage specifics (shelf-life, calories per dollar, e.g. LDS/Church food-storage guidance) were not retrieved.

The EA and x-risk community, led by 80,000 Hours, recommends that individuals contribute through **(1) careers in government, research, communications/advocacy, or field-building (including earning to give); (2) donations to vetted GCR funds and organisations; and (3) advocacy**. ## Q8. Which actions are robust (help across many scenarios) vs scenario-specific? Any expert cost-effectiveness rankings?

### Takeaway
No source ranked personal prep actions by cost-effectiveness across catastrophic risks. Qualitatively, though, official sources (US, Finland) and x-risk thinkers point to the same **robust core**:
1. A water, food, medicines and light/radio kit for at least 72h, ideally 2 weeks.
2. An emergency fund, savings and some cash.
3. An alert and communications plan.
4. A reusable P100 respirator plus goggles.
5. Know-how for sheltering in place (sealing rooms, turning off HVAC).
6. Community ties and psychological resilience.
7. Offline data backups.
8. Staying healthy and avoiding accident risk.

Scenario-specific, high-cost actions (relocation to the Southern Hemisphere or rural areas, months of food, AI-tilted portfolios, far-UVC) only make sense under high priors on specific risks.

### Cited Findings
- Ready.gov frames its kit as "all hazards" and keeps it per-location (home, work, car). The nuclear, space-weather and heat pages all point back to "Build a Kit" and "family communications plan." — [Ready.gov Kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit); [Space Weather](https://www.ready.gov/space-weather); [Radiation](https://www.ready.gov/nuclear-explosion); [Heat](https://www.ready.gov/heat)
- Finland's 72h programme bundles power outage, water, food, sheltering indoors, infectious disease, cybersecurity and psychological resilience into one home-preparedness standard. — [72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/en/)
- Ready.gov's "shelter in place" kit items (plastic sheeting, duct tape) and its radiation guidance (turn off HVAC that draws outside air, close windows) match Finland's "shut down ventilation and seal off windows." — [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/nuclear-explosion); [72tuntia.fi](https://72tuntia.fi/en/)
- Todd: actions should be "fine if timelines turn out to be much longer". He says his list mostly is, "provided you don't take them to the extreme." — [Benjamin Todd](https://benjamintodd.substack.com/p/how-can-an-ordinary-person-prepare)
- Zvi: "There are not good ways to sacrifice quite a lot of utility in the normal case, and in exchange get good experiential value in unusual cases." Debt and consumption-shifting "suffer rapidly decreasing marginal returns." — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-practical-advice-for-the-worried)
- The International AI Safety Report 2026 endorses a "defence-in-depth" model, using the analogy that "vaccines, masks, and hand-washing... can reduce the risk of infection substantially in combination, even though none of these methods are 100% effective on their own." — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/)
